Does extra energy create a new cellular state?

Energy-dependent switching and saddle-node boundaries

CCBS · EST 5053 · extension mini-essay · Lectures 9–10 · Week 5 · 2026-10-12

The claim

Energy input alone does not create an attractor. In a pre-existing positive-feedback switch it can move a saddle-node boundary or change hysteresis. A monotone one-dimensional energy pool must not generate stable–unstable–stable roots merely because its source is stronger.

1Problem

A changed output level is not automatically a changed state space.

I use one commitment switch rather than mixing bistability, oscillations and adaptation. First, E is externally clamped so the bifurcation is identifiable; only later should a dynamic shared pool or target-local route be added. A new state means a reproducible stable fixed point or hysteresis region absent from baseline, not a faster response or a shifted single root.

2Minimal model

dx/dt=[k0+Vfbf(E)xn/(Kxn+xn)](1−x)−koffx;
f(E)=E/(KE+E).

The no-feedback null has n=1 or Vfb=0. The illustrative switch uses n=3, k0=0.01, Vfb=30, Kx=0.4, koff=0.5 and KE=1. A later dynamic-pool model would add dE/dt=Jaux−νvcommit−ℓE, but it is not used to infer the first diagram.

3Continuation assay and controls

For each E, solve F(x;E)=0 from multiple initial conditions and classify roots by F′(x*) (negative is stable). Sweep E upward and downward; near a candidate boundary, run at least 1,000 stochastic trajectories. Report root count, positions, eigenvalue/slope, thresholds and hysteresis width.

4Illustrative result

At E=0.5, the synthetic model gives x*=0.023, 0.043 and 0.949; slopes are −0.26, +0.31 and −9.71, so low and high roots are stable and the middle root unstable. At E=1.0 only x*=0.966 remains. The low-state saddle-node lies between E=0.7 and 0.8 in this grid and must be localized by continuation.

5Falsification, limits and next test

Falsifiable prediction

For n=1, Vfb=0 or any strictly monotone addition/removal model, an E sweep must not produce stable–unstable–stable roots. If it does, audit the equations, boundary, root finder and units. For n>1, a state change should occur only when energy-dependent feedback crosses a saddle-node condition.

Finite observation windows can confuse metastable dwell with stable attraction; use slopes, dwell distributions and perturbation recovery. The Hill term is phenomenological, and the model omits growth dilution, membrane space, ROS, oscillators and adaptation. The next test is to add dynamic E only after the clamped-E diagram is reproducible, comparing shared and target-local routes.
