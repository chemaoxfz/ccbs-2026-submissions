Week 6 — Extension: a reporter can move the threshold it reads

Author: Kehan Lang · Week 6 (2026-10-26) · CCBS 2026

One sentence: When a finite reporter binds the regulator it measures, the reporter is not a passive readout: its abundance shifts the total input required to cross the decision threshold.

The point I am adding

Lecture 12 builds a molecular threshold unit in which free protein S represses an output reporter. The calculation is deliberately made with a rare reporter, so that the reporter does not appreciably change the allocation of the proteins that implement the comparison. That is a useful approximation, but it hides a design condition: a reporter can only report a threshold without moving it if its binding load is negligible compared with the relevant molecular pools.

Here I relax the rare-reporter approximation by allowing a finite reporter pool. If the input is controlled as a total amount of repressor, how does reporter sequestration change the dose at which the reporter crosses a chosen activity threshold? For one equilibrium binding pair, the answer is exact: the threshold shifts linearly with reporter abundance.

Work it

Let free repressor S bind an available reporter site P to form an inactive complex C. The reporter's unbound fraction is its normalized activity, r = P/qP. Binding and conservation give

S + P ⇌ C,   KC = SP,   qS = S + C,   qP = P + C.

Here K is the dissociation concentration, while qS and qP are the total repressor and reporter-site concentrations. Eliminating the free pools gives a quadratic for the bound reporter:

C2 − (qS + qP + K)C + qSqP = 0.

The physical root is the smaller one, because it lies between zero and the smaller total. If the reporter is rare, qP → 0, free repressor approaches qS, and the usual unloaded response is recovered:

runloaded(qS) = K/(K + qS).

For a loaded reporter, it is simpler to locate a decision boundary by specifying the threshold first. At r = θ, free reporter is P = θqP, so the bound fraction is C = (1 − θ)qP. The binding equation then requires free repressor S = K(1 − θ)/θ. Adding the free and bound repressor gives the total repressor threshold:

qS,θ = K(1 − θ)/θ + (1 − θ)qP.

The first term is the unloaded threshold. The second is repressor captured by the reporter. At the common half-activity readout θ = 1/2, the boundary becomes qS,1/2 = K + qP/2. Thus every additional two reporter sites per concentration unit require one additional repressor unit to reach half repression.

Interpretation

The shift is not a failure of equilibrium binding; it is a conservation effect. A total repressor intervention must supply both the free repressor that determines the reporter occupancy and the repressor captured in complexes. Consequently, the phrase “the reporter sees the input” is incomplete when input means a total concentration. The reporter changes the free concentration that it is intended to sense.

This gives an operational criterion for the rare-reporter approximation. Near a threshold θ, the loading correction is small only when (1 − θ)qP is small compared with the unloaded threshold K(1 − θ)/θ; equivalently, qP ≪ K/θ. The condition is most demanding for a high-activity threshold. A circuit designer can reduce loading by lowering reporter abundance, weakening its binding, or using a downstream readout whose occupancy does not materially deplete S.

What I would argue against / where this breaks

This model has one binding site, one repressor species, and equilibrium allocation before the readout is interpreted. It treats the reporter-site total as fixed and equates its unbound fraction with activity. Production, degradation, cooperative promoter binding, multiple reporter sites, and slow binding kinetics would require an enlarged model. The result also concerns total repressor control. If an external reservoir instead fixes free S, adding reporter changes the amount that must be supplied by the reservoir but does not move the free-concentration threshold. Finally, a reporter may be deliberately designed to load the system; in that case the shift is a mechanism, not a measurement error.

// Concentrations are normalized by K.
function complex(qS, qP) {
  const B = qS + qP + 1;
  return (B - Math.sqrt(B * B - 4 * qS * qP)) / 2;
}
function activity(qS, qP) { return 1 - complex(qS, qP) / qP; }
function totalBoundary(qP, theta) { return (1 - theta) / theta + (1 - theta) * qP; }
