Week 3 — Extension: measuring intrinsic vs extrinsic noise from the average distance between data points

Author: Álvaro Bátrez · Week 3 (lectures 5–6) · 2026-09-17 · CCBS 2026

One sentence: if each cell carries two identical reporters, the average distance between the two reporters inside one cell measures intrinsic noise, the average distance of the pair means from the population mean measures extrinsic noise — and a from-scratch simulation shows the two numbers separate cleanly on dispersed vs non-dispersed data.

The point I am adding

Intrinsic noise is the randomness a gene generates by its own small numbers — promoter switching, birth and death of individual molecules (lecture 5's master equation and Gillespie). It is uncorrelated between genes. Extrinsic noise is everything the cell imposes on all its genes at once — growth phase, ribosome abundance, cell size, upstream regulators. It is correlated across genes. The lecture left open how to actually measure the split; here is the distance-based version of the classic two-reporter method (Elowitz et al. 2002): express two identical, independent reporters C₁, C₂ in the same cell. Whatever moves them together is extrinsic; whatever moves them apart is intrinsic.

The expressions

For n cells with reporters C₁ⁱ, C₂ⁱ and grand mean ⟨C⟩, define the average within-pair distance (intrinsic) and the average distance of pair means from the grand mean (extrinsic):

dint = (1/n) Σ |C₁ⁱ − C₂ⁱ|,    dext = (1/n) Σ |mⁱ − ⟨C⟩|,  with mⁱ = (C₁ⁱ + C₂ⁱ)/2

The variance version (law of total variance, Var(C) = σ²ext + σ²int) gives the standard normalized noise coefficients:

η²int = ⟨(C₁ − C₂)²⟩ / (2⟨C⟩²),    η²ext = Var(m) / ⟨C⟩²

Ratio rule of thumb: dint ≫ dext → intrinsic dominates; dext ≫ dint → extrinsic dominates.

Work it — two synthetic datasets

I simulated n = 300 cells, each with two reporters (mean 100 a.u.): a shared lognormal cell factor (extrinsic, c.v. 45% vs 3%) and independent gamma fluctuations per reporter (intrinsic, c.v. 5% vs 3%).

The program

Self-written (NumPy, Matplotlib). Input: two arrays of reporter measurements from the same cells. Output: the two average distances and the normalized coefficients.

import numpy as np

def distance_decomp(c1, c2):
    ## c1, c2: reporter 1 and reporter 2, one measurement per cell
    n = len(c1)
    grand = np.mean(np.concatenate([c1, c2]))
    d_int = np.mean(np.abs(c1 - c2))                 # within-cell distance
    m = (c1 + c2)/2                                  # pair mean
    d_ext = np.mean(np.abs(m - grand))               # between-cell distance
    # variance version (Elowitz et al. 2002), normalized
    eta2_int = np.mean((c1 - c2)**2) / (2*grand**2)
    eta2_ext = np.var(m) / grand**2
    # *correction: Var(m) also contains half the intrinsic variance, so
    # when intrinsic is large subtract it:  eta2_ext -= eta2_int/2
    eta2_ext_corrected = eta2_ext - eta2_int/2
    return dict(d_int=d_int, d_ext=d_ext,
                eta_int=np.sqrt(eta2_int),
                eta_ext=np.sqrt(max(eta2_ext_corrected, 0.0)))

# --- synthetic test data (my simulation) ---
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
def sample(n=300, mean=100.0, cv_ext=0.45, cv_int=0.05):
    lam = mean * rng.lognormal(0.0, cv_ext, n)       # extrinsic cell factor
    c1 = lam * rng.gamma(1.0/cv_int**2, cv_int**2, n)
    c2 = lam * rng.gamma(1.0/cv_int**2, cv_int**2, n)
    return c1, c2

print(distance_decomp(*sample(cv_ext=0.45, cv_int=0.05)))  # dispersed  -> extrinsic
print(distance_decomp(*sample(cv_ext=0.03, cv_int=0.03)))  # quiet      -> neither
print(distance_decomp(*sample(cv_ext=0.03, cv_int=0.35)))  # intrinsic-dominated

What I would argue against / where this breaks

The absolute-distance version is robust but not the standard estimator — dext mixes in half the intrinsic noise (marked * in the table; the program subtracts it). The method also assumes the two reporters are truly identical and independent: any differential regulation, maturation delay, or measurement noise specific to one color is silently booked as "intrinsic". And extrinsic here means "shared by the two reporters," not "not stochastic" — a slow fluctuating upstream regulator is extrinsic by this definition yet perfectly amenable to the master-equation treatment of lecture 5. With one reporter per cell the split is impossible in principle; the pairing, not the averaging, is what buys the decomposition.
