Week 3 — Extension: Under a periodic drive, the time average over a whole cycle need not equal the fixed-phase ensemble

Author: Zheng Haobo · Week 3 (2026-09-21) · CCBS 2026

One sentence: For a birth–removal process whose addition channel is driven periodically as b(t)=b0[1+εsin(ωt)], the residence-time average taken along a single path over complete cycles estimates a "cycle mixture" — the phase average 1T∫0Tp(t)dt, one number — whereas the threshold probability of the fixed-phase ensemble, p(φ)=Pr{Poisson(mper(φ))≥θ}, is a function of phase. The two agree only in the degenerate case ε=0, or after the phase curve is itself averaged over phase; so there is no reason for a single-path estimate of the former to reproduce the latter.

The point I am adding

The estimator contract given in Lecture 5 separates two sampling operations: M independent paths sampled at the same physical instant estimate the fixed-time ensemble E[g(N(t))] and need no stationarity or ergodicity assumption; a single path weighted by the residence times of its holding intervals gives a time average, and reading that as a stationary ensemble needs an ergodicity assumption. Every worked record in the lecture — the four-minute record (N̄₄ = 2.05, 1̄N≥3 = 0.05), the 20–30 minute window of the live demo, the 5000-path numerical comparison — is a time-homogeneous, constant-propensity case. At the same time, the exactness of the direct method rests on a single hypothesis: propensities stay fixed between reaction events — the state does not change between events, so the propensity does not change, so the waiting time comes from a fixed distribution, and only then is the two-uniform loop exact.

This essay adds exactly one thing: an exogenous periodic drive on the addition channel, b(t)=b₀[1+ε·sin(ωt)], while removal stays γN. That moves two layers at once. Sampling layer: the addition propensity changes between events, so the "fixed distribution" premise fails and event times must be found by solving the integrated-hazard equation (a standard repair in the literature, not a contribution here). Estimation layer: the process no longer has a single stationary distribution, so the threshold probability becomes a function of phase, p(φ), while the complete-cycle time average is "one number" whose expectation is the phase mixture by linearity alone — that step needs no cycloergodicity assumption. The two estimators therefore have different targets: one converges to the mixture (one number), the other gives a phase curve. Treating the former as the latter is precisely the estimator–target mismatch that the L5 contract warns about, now carrying periodic structure. The autonomous periodic dynamics of L4 (repressilator, Poincaré–Bendixson periodic orbits) and the stationary estimators of L5 are background; the new extension is the phase-conditioned comparison under a periodic drive.

Stated boundaries: the claim is only that the two "need not agree." At ε=0 they coincide (used below as a baseline check), and averaging the phase curve over phase also gives the mixture; b(t)≥b₀(1−ε)>0 keeps the process from ever absorbing, so this is the benign case — no ergodicity failure is claimed here, unlike the core autocatalytic counterexample (ensemble mean identically 1, while the path mean tends to 0 along almost every path). Every number below comes from actually running the code embedded in this page (seeds fixed), and Monte Carlo errors are reported as they are.

Work it

The model and the two estimators are stated as a six-line contract (time in min, rates in min⁻¹; the code is at the end of this section, complete and runnable, with no external dependencies):

  • State. N∈{0,1,2,…}, the molecule count.
  • Events. addition ∅→N with a₊(t)=b₀[1+ε·sin(ωt)]; removal N→∅ with a₋(t,N)=γN; total hazard a₀=a₊+a₋.
  • Path (sampling repair). The next event time τ solves ∫tt+τa₀(s,N)ds=−ln u₁, with closed form
H(τ)=(b0+γN)τ+b0εω[cos(ωt)−cos(ω(t+τ))]

ε<1 guarantees a₀>0 and a strictly increasing H, so the root is found by safeguarded Newton; the event channel is drawn with probability a₊(t+τ)/a₀(t+τ) — conditional on the event time, the channel odds equal the hazard ratio at that instant.

  • Observable and estimators. Y=1{N≥θ}, θ=4. A (residence): after a burn-in of 10 complete cycles (80 min≫1/γ), count the threshold occupancy with the actual holding intervals, so that any interval crossing a phase or cycle boundary is credited to the part that lies inside, over K=40 complete cycles; M=2000 independent paths give one value each, reported as mean±SE (the SE comes from the number of paths, not the number of events). B (fixed-phase snapshot): every path is sampled exactly once per recorded cycle at phase φᵢ=2πi/8, giving a per-path frequency, and then a mean±SE over the M paths.
  • Parameters. b₀=2, ε=0.6, ω=2π/8 (period 8 min), γ=1, N(0)=0, seed=20260915.

Analytic benchmark (specific to immigration–death, and exact): N(t) stays Poisson, its mean m(t) solves m′(t)=b(t)−γm(t), and the periodic steady state is the closed form

mper(t)=b0γ+b0εγ2+ω2sin(ωt−arctanωγ)

The code integrates m(0)=mper(0) by RK4 over 40 cycles and compares the result with this closed form (maximum deviation 1.2e-12), and the Poisson survival function is checked against the hand-computed Pr{Pois(2)≥4}=0.14288. Both p(φ) and the phase mixture can therefore be computed exactly, independently of the simulation — each estimator's target gets its own reference answer.

Results (actually run, about 3.20 million events per experiment). ε=0 baseline: analytic p=0.1429; A=0.1425±0.0005, 0.7 SE away from the analytic mixture; all 8 phase snapshots lie within 1.9 SE of the analytic value — the three objects coincide, and with the drive switched off there is no phase structure. ε=0.6 main experiment: p(φ) runs from a peak of 0.3384±0.0017 (φ=3π/4) to a trough of 0.0227±0.0005 (φ=7π/4), a ratio of about 15; A=0.1619±0.0005, only 0.9 SE away from the analytic mixture 0.1624. But A differs from the peak by about 344 SE (measured in A's SE) and from the trough by about 272 SE; A also differs from the value obtained by freezing the drive at its mean, b₀/γ, which gives 0.1429 (about 38 SE — p is convex in m, so by Jensen's inequality the cycle mixture lies above the value at the mean). The peak and the trough are two points on the fixed-phase ensemble curve; A is the phase average of that curve. Two objects, and numerically there is no reason for them to be equal.

Complete source of the artifact made for this essay (simulate.py, Python 3 standard library, runs directly and reproduces every number in the text) 
"""Periodic drive on a birth-removal count process: is the complete-cycle
time average the same number as the fixed-phase ensemble threshold probability?

Model (one state, two channels)
    N(t) in {0, 1, 2, ...}
    addition  -> N+1   a_+(t)   = b0 * (1 + eps*sin(omega*t))    [1/min]
    removal   -> N-1   a_-(t,N) = gamma * N                      [1/min]
    total     a0(t,N)  = a_+(t) + a_-(t,N)

Because a_+ depends on absolute time, propensities are NOT fixed between
events, so the classic two-uniform loop (fixed exponential waiting time) is
not exact here.  The exact next-event time tau solves the integrated-hazard
equation  H(tau) = -ln(u1),  with the closed form

    H(tau) = (b0 + gamma*N)*tau
             + (b0*eps/omega)*(cos(omega*t) - cos(omega*(t+tau))) ,

strictly increasing in tau because a0 > 0 (0 < eps < 1).  Each event solves
H(tau) = E by safeguarded Newton iterations, then the channel is drawn with
probability a_+(t+tau)/a0(t+tau): conditional on the event time, the channel
odds equal the hazard ratio at that instant.

Estimators of the threshold indicator Y = 1{N >= theta}
  A. single-path residence-time average over K complete cycles after burn-in
     (weights are elapsed times of holding intervals, clipped at cycle
     boundaries); the ensemble of M such path values gives mean and standard
     error over independent paths.
  B. fixed-phase snapshot ensemble: every path is sampled stroboscopically at
     the same phase of the drive, once per recorded cycle; the per-path
     frequency over K cycles gives one value per phase, and the mean and
     standard error are taken over the M independent paths.

Exact analytic benchmark for this immigration-death process: if N(t0) is
Poisson then N(t) stays Poisson with mean m(t) solving m' = b(t) - gamma*m.
The periodic steady state is closed form,

    m_per(t) = b0/gamma + (b0*eps/sqrt(gamma^2 + omega^2)) * sin(omega*t - delta),
    delta = atan(omega/gamma),

so the fixed-phase ensemble threshold probability is p(phi) = Pr{Poisson(
m_per(phi)) >= theta} (phase phi = omega*t) and the cycle mixture is the
phase average of p over one period.  The simulator is checked against this
benchmark.  Pure standard library; deterministic given SEED.  Running this
file prints the self-test results and every number quoted in the essay;
run_all() returns the numbers and the two SVG figure strings for inlining.
"""

import math
import random
import time

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# parameters (time in minutes, rates in 1/min, counts are molecules)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEED     = 20260915               # base seed; per-path seeds derive from it
B0       = 2.0                    # mean addition rate b0           [1/min]
EPS      = 0.6                    # drive depth, 0 < eps < 1 keeps b(t) > 0
OMEGA    = 2.0 * math.pi / 8.0    # angular frequency; period = 8 min
GAMMA    = 1.0                    # per-molecule removal rate       [1/min]
N0       = 0                      # initial count N(0)
BURN_IN  = 10                     # burn-in cycles (80 min >> 1/gamma = 1 min)
N_CYCLES = 40                     # recorded complete cycles per path
N_PATHS  = 2000                   # independent paths M
THETA    = 4                      # threshold in Y = 1{N >= theta}
N_PHASES = 8                      # snapshot phases per cycle
N_BINS   = 24                     # phase bins for the residence cross-check
SIMPSON_N = 4000                  # even subinterval count for the mixture integral


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# model and exact time-dependent SSA
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def b_drive(t, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA):
    """Time-dependent addition propensity b(t)."""
    return b0 * (1.0 + eps * math.sin(omega * t))


def a_total(t, n, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA):
    """Total propensity a0(t, N)."""
    return b0 * (1.0 + eps * math.sin(omega * t)) + gamma * n


def hazard_cum(tau, t, n, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA):
    """Integral of a0 from t to t+tau (closed form)."""
    return ((b0 + gamma * n) * tau
            + (b0 * eps / omega)
            * (math.cos(omega * t) - math.cos(omega * (t + tau))))


def solve_tau(E, t, n, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA):
    """Solve hazard_cum(tau) = E by safeguarded Newton (bisection bracket).

    H is continuous, H(0) = 0 and strictly increasing (a0 > 0), so the root
    is unique.  The bracket upper end uses the bound |cos| <= 1.
    """
    r = b0 + gamma * n
    w = omega
    amp = b0 * eps / w
    wt = w * t
    lo, hi = 0.0, (E + 2.0 * amp) / r
    tau = E / r                               # eps = 0 guess
    for _ in range(60):
        err = r * tau + amp * (math.cos(wt) - math.cos(wt + w * tau)) - E
        if err > 0.0:
            hi = tau
        else:
            lo = tau
        d = r + b0 * eps * math.sin(wt + w * tau)   # dH/dtau = a0(t+tau) > 0
        new = tau - err / d if d > 0.0 else 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        if not (lo < new < hi):
            new = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        done = abs(new - tau) <= 1e-11 * max(1.0, new)
        tau = new
        if done:
            break
    return tau


def simulate_path(rng, t_end, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA, n0=N0):
    """One path on [0, t_end]; returns the event list [(t_j, N after event)].

    The state is constant between events, so each holding interval is
    (t_j, t_{j+1}, N_j); the initial state n0 holds on [0, t_1).
    """
    events = []
    t = 0.0
    n = n0
    while True:
        E = -math.log(1.0 - rng.random())     # u1 in (0, 1], E = -ln u1
        tau = solve_tau(E, t, n, b0, eps, omega, gamma)
        t2 = t + tau
        if t2 > t_end:
            break
        # channel: addition wins with probability a_+(t2)/a0(t2)
        if rng.random() * a_total(t2, n, b0, eps, omega, gamma) \
                < b_drive(t2, b0, eps, omega):
            n += 1
        else:
            n -= 1        # unreachable at n = 0, where a_- = 0
        events.append((t2, n))
        t = t2
    return events


def iter_intervals(events, n0=N0):
    """Yield (t_start, t_end, n) over the piecewise-constant path."""
    t_prev = 0.0
    n = n0
    for t2, n2 in events:
        yield t_prev, t2, n
        t_prev = t2
        n = n2
    yield t_prev, math.inf, n


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# estimators
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def residence_average(events, rec_start, rec_end, theta, n_bins,
                      period):
    """Estimator A on one path.

    Only the part of each holding interval inside [rec_start, rec_end] (K
    complete cycles) contributes.  Returns the overall residence fraction
    with N >= theta, plus the same fraction per phase bin (pooled by
    cycle-relative phase) for the cross-check against the analytic curve.
    """
    num = den = 0.0
    num_bin = [0.0] * n_bins
    den_bin = [0.0] * n_bins
    bw = period / n_bins
    for t1, t2, n in iter_intervals(events):
        if t1 >= rec_end:
            break
        a = t1 if t1 > rec_start else rec_start
        b = t2 if t2 < rec_end else rec_end
        if b <= a:
            continue
        dur = b - a
        den += dur
        hit = n >= theta
        if hit:
            num += dur
        k_lo = int((a - rec_start) / bw)
        k_hi = int((b - rec_start) / bw)
        for k in range(k_lo, k_hi + 1):
            blo = rec_start + k * bw
            ov = (b if b < blo + bw else blo + bw) - (a if a > blo else blo)
            if ov > 0.0:
                j = k % n_bins
                den_bin[j] += ov
                if hit:
                    num_bin[j] += ov
    return num / den, num_bin, den_bin


def stroboscopic_frequencies(events, rec_start, n_cycles, theta, n0,
                             n_phases, period):
    """Estimator B on one path: for each snapshot phase phi_i = 2*pi*i/n_phases,
    the fraction of the n_cycles recorded cycles in which the state covering
    that instant has N >= theta.  Returns counts[i]/n_cycles."""
    targets = sorted((rec_start + (k + i / n_phases) * period, i)
                     for i in range(n_phases) for k in range(n_cycles))
    counts = [0] * n_phases
    ivals = iter_intervals(events, n0)
    t1, t2, n = next(ivals)
    for t_tgt, i in targets:
        while t_tgt >= t2:
            t1, t2, n = next(ivals)
        if n >= theta:
            counts[i] += 1
    return [c / n_cycles for c in counts]


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# analytic benchmark (exact for immigration-death)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def m_periodic(t, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA):
    """Closed-form periodic steady state of m' = b(t) - gamma*m."""
    amp = b0 * eps / math.sqrt(gamma * gamma + omega * omega)
    delta = math.atan2(omega, gamma)
    return b0 / gamma + amp * math.sin(omega * t - delta)


def m_periodic_rk4(n_out=101, n_cycles=40, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA,
                   gamma=GAMMA, steps_per_cycle=2000):
    """Integrate m' = b(t) - gamma*m by RK4 from m(0) = m_per(0); return
    [(t, m)] at n_out evenly spaced times on [0, n_cycles*T].  Used only to
    check the closed form."""
    T = 2.0 * math.pi / omega
    h = T / steps_per_cycle
    total = n_cycles * steps_per_cycle
    stride = max(1, total // (n_out - 1))
    m = m_periodic(0.0, b0, eps, omega, gamma)
    out = [(0.0, m)]
    for s in range(1, total + 1):
        t = (s - 1) * h
        k1 = b_drive(t, b0, eps, omega) - gamma * m
        k2 = b_drive(t + h / 2, b0, eps, omega) - gamma * (m + h / 2 * k1)
        k3 = b_drive(t + h / 2, b0, eps, omega) - gamma * (m + h / 2 * k2)
        k4 = b_drive(t + h, b0, eps, omega) - gamma * (m + h * k3)
        m += h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4)
        if s % stride == 0:
            out.append((s * h, m))
    return out


def poisson_sf(m, k):
    """Pr{Poisson(m) >= k} for integer k >= 0, by direct pmf summation."""
    if m <= 0.0:
        return 0.0
    s = 0.0
    for j in range(k):
        s += math.exp(j * math.log(m) - m - math.lgamma(j + 1.0))
    return max(0.0, 1.0 - s)


def p_phase(t, theta=THETA, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA):
    """Fixed-phase ensemble threshold probability Pr{N(t) >= theta}."""
    return poisson_sf(m_periodic(t, b0, eps, omega, gamma), theta)


def mixture_analytic(theta=THETA, b0=B0, eps=EPS, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA,
                     n=SIMPSON_N):
    """Cycle-time mixture: (1/T) * integral of p_phase over one period."""
    T = 2.0 * math.pi / omega
    h = T / n
    s = p_phase(0.0, theta, b0, eps, omega, gamma) \
        + p_phase(T, theta, b0, eps, omega, gamma)
    for i in range(1, n):
        s += (4.0 if i % 2 else 2.0) * p_phase(i * h, theta, b0, eps, omega, gamma)
    return s * h / 3.0 / T


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# experiment driver
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def run_experiment(eps, seed=SEED, n_paths=N_PATHS, burn_in=BURN_IN,
                   n_cycles=N_CYCLES, b0=B0, omega=OMEGA, gamma=GAMMA,
                   n0=N0, theta=THETA, with_example=False):
    """Run the full estimator comparison for one drive depth.

    Returns the analytic targets, the two simulated estimators (mean and
    standard error over the M independent path-level values), and the pooled
    per-bin residence curve used as an extra cross-check.
    """
    T = 2.0 * math.pi / omega
    rec_start = burn_in * T
    rec_end = (burn_in + n_cycles) * T
    phases = [2.0 * math.pi * i / N_PHASES for i in range(N_PHASES)]

    # analytic targets as functions of phase phi = omega*t
    amp = b0 * eps / math.sqrt(gamma * gamma + omega * omega)
    delta = math.atan2(omega, gamma)
    m_at = [b0 / gamma + amp * math.sin(phi - delta) for phi in phases]
    p_at = [poisson_sf(m, theta) for m in m_at]
    mix = mixture_analytic(theta, b0, eps, omega, gamma)
    p_curve = [(2.0 * math.pi * i / 200.0,
                p_phase(2.0 * math.pi * i / 200.0 / omega, theta, b0, eps,
                        omega, gamma)) for i in range(200)]

    # simulation: M independent paths
    a_vals = []
    snap_vals = [[] for _ in range(N_PHASES)]
    bin_num = [0.0] * N_BINS
    bin_den = [0.0] * N_BINS
    tot_events = 0
    example = None
    clock = time.perf_counter()
    for i in range(n_paths):
        s = (seed * 1000003 + 7919 * i + int(round(eps * 1000)) * 104729) \
            % (2 ** 63)
        rng = random.Random(s)
        ev = simulate_path(rng, rec_end, b0, eps, omega, gamma, n0)
        tot_events += len(ev)
        a, nb, db = residence_average(ev, rec_start, rec_end, theta,
                                      N_BINS, T)
        a_vals.append(a)
        for j in range(N_BINS):
            bin_num[j] += nb[j]
            bin_den[j] += db[j]
        freqs = stroboscopic_frequencies(ev, rec_start, n_cycles, theta, n0,
                                         N_PHASES, T)
        for j, f in enumerate(freqs):
            snap_vals[j].append(f)
        if with_example and i == 0:
            example = {"intervals": [(t1, t2, n)
                                     for t1, t2, n in iter_intervals(ev, n0)
                                     if t2 > rec_start
                                     and t1 < rec_start + 2.0 * T]}
    runtime = time.perf_counter() - clock

    def mean_sd_se(xs):
        m = sum(xs) / len(xs)
        var = sum((x - m) ** 2 for x in xs) / (len(xs) - 1)
        return m, math.sqrt(var), math.sqrt(var / len(xs))

    a_mean, a_sd, a_se = mean_sd_se(a_vals)
    snap_mean, snap_se = [], []
    for j in range(N_PHASES):
        m, _, se = mean_sd_se(snap_vals[j])
        snap_mean.append(m)
        snap_se.append(se)
    bin_p = [bin_num[j] / bin_den[j] for j in range(N_BINS)]
    # cross-check: pooled residence curve vs analytic p at the bin centres
    bin_ref = [p_phase(rec_start + (j + 0.5) * (T / N_BINS), theta, b0, eps,
                       omega, gamma) for j in range(N_BINS)]
    max_dev = max(abs(bin_p[j] - bin_ref[j]) for j in range(N_BINS))
    return {
        "params": {"b0": b0, "eps": eps, "omega": omega, "gamma": gamma,
                   "n0": n0, "theta": theta, "burn_in": burn_in,
                   "n_cycles": n_cycles, "n_paths": n_paths, "period": T,
                   "seed": seed, "n_phases": N_PHASES, "n_bins": N_BINS},
        "analytic": {"mbar": b0 / gamma, "amp": amp, "delta": delta,
                     "phases": phases, "m_at_phases": m_at,
                     "p_at_phases": p_at, "mixture": mix, "p_curve": p_curve},
        "sim": {"A_mean": a_mean, "A_sd": a_sd, "A_se": a_se,
                "snap_mean": snap_mean, "snap_se": snap_se,
                "bin_p": bin_p, "bin_ref": bin_ref, "max_dev_bin": max_dev,
                "n_events": tot_events, "runtime_s": runtime},
        "example": example,
    }


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# self-tests (deterministic)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def self_tests():
    """Return a list of (name, passed, detail) for the numerical blocks."""
    tests = []

    # Poisson survival against a hand-checked reference value
    ref = 0.1428765395   # Pr{Pois(2) >= 4} = 1 - e^-2 (1 + 2 + 2 + 4/3)
    val = poisson_sf(2.0, 4)
    tests.append(("poisson_sf(2,4) reference", abs(val - ref) < 1e-9,
                  f"got {val:.10f}, ref {ref}"))

    # closed-form periodic mean vs RK4 integration of m' = b - gamma m
    num = m_periodic_rk4()
    dev = max(abs(m_num - m_periodic(t)) for t, m_num in num)
    tests.append(("m_per closed form vs RK4", dev < 1e-8, f"max dev {dev:.2e}"))

    # root solver: residual and positivity at random states
    rng = random.Random(20260915)
    worst = 0.0
    for _ in range(2000):
        t = rng.uniform(0.0, 8.0)
        n = rng.randrange(0, 8)
        E = 4.0 * rng.expovariate(0.25) * 0.25
        tau = solve_tau(E, t, n)
        res = abs(hazard_cum(tau, t, n) - E) / max(E, 1e-12)
        worst = max(worst, res)
        if a_total(t + tau, n) <= 0.0:
            worst = 1.0
    tests.append(("solve_tau residual", worst < 1e-9,
                  f"worst rel residual {worst:.2e}"))

    # residence accounting: total recorded time must equal n_cycles * period
    T = 2.0 * math.pi / OMEGA
    rng = random.Random(7)
    ev = simulate_path(rng, (BURN_IN + N_CYCLES) * T)
    _, nb, db = residence_average(ev, BURN_IN * T, (BURN_IN + N_CYCLES) * T,
                                  THETA, N_BINS, T)
    err = abs(sum(db) - N_CYCLES * T) / (N_CYCLES * T)
    tests.append(("residence time accounting", err < 1e-9,
                  f"rel err {err:.2e}"))
    return tests

    return f'<path d="{d}" class="{cls}"/>'


def _dots(pts, cls, r=2.4):
    return "".join(f'<circle cx="{x:.2f}" cy="{y:.2f}" r="{r}" class="{cls}"/>'
                   for x, y in pts)


def _panel_a(res, x0, y0, w, h):
    """Left panel: total hazard a0(t, N(t)) along one example path with events
    marked.  With eps > 0 the curve keeps moving between events; with eps = 0
    it stays flat between events (the frozen-clock premise holds)."""
    P = res["params"]
    T = P["period"]
    rec_start = P["burn_in"] * T
    tmax = 2.0 * T
    X = lambda t: x0 + (t - rec_start) / tmax * w
    Y = lambda v: y0 + h - v / vmax * h

    # hazard curve, sampled finely inside each holding interval (two passes:
    # first collect raw samples and the y-range, then map to the panel)
    samples, dots = [], []
    for t1, t2, n in res["example"]["intervals"]:
        a1, a2 = max(t1, rec_start), min(t2, rec_start + tmax)
        if a2 <= a1:
            continue
        nseg = max(2, int((a2 - a1) / 0.04))
        for i in range(nseg + 1):
            tt = a1 + (a2 - a1) * i / nseg
            samples.append((tt, a_total(tt, n, P["b0"], P["eps"],
                                        P["omega"], P["gamma"])))
        if rec_start < t2 <= rec_start + tmax:
            dots.append((t2, a_total(t2, n, P["b0"], P["eps"], P["omega"],
                                     P["gamma"])))
    vmax = math.ceil(max(v for _, v in samples) * 1.02)
    step = next(s for s in (1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0) if vmax / s <= 6.0)

    parts = []
    parts.append(_ln(x0, y0 + h, x0 + w, y0 + h, "ax"))
    parts.append(_ln(x0, y0, x0, y0 + h, "ax"))
    v = step
    while v <= vmax + 1e-9:
        parts.append(_ln(x0, Y(v), x0 + w, Y(v), "tick"))
        parts.append(_txt(x0 - 6, Y(v) + 4, f"{v:g}", "axtext", "end"))
        v += step
    for tt in range(0, int(tmax) + 1, 4):
        parts.append(_ln(X(rec_start + tt), y0 + h, X(rec_start + tt),
                         y0 + h + 4, "ax"))
        parts.append(_txt(X(rec_start + tt), y0 + h + 16, f"{tt}", "axtext"))
    parts.append(_ln(X(rec_start + T), y0, X(rec_start + T), y0 + h, "cycle"))
    parts.append(_txt(x0 + w / 2, y0 + h + 32, "t (min)", "axtext"))
    yx, yy = x0 - 34, y0 + h / 2
    parts.append(_txt(yx, yy, "a\u2080(t) (1/min)", "axtext", "middle",
                      f"rotate(-90 {yx:.1f} {yy:.1f})"))
    parts.append(_txt(x0 + 2, y0 - 10, "(a) total hazard along one path, "
                      "with events", "ptitle", "start"))
    parts.append(_poly([(X(t), Y(v)) for t, v in samples], "haz"))
    parts.append(_dots([(X(t), Y(v)) for t, v in dots], "evt"))
    return "".join(parts)


def _panel_b(res, x0, y0, w, h, ymax):
    """Right panel: phase-resolved threshold probability.  Analytic p(phi)
    curve, fixed-phase snapshot ensemble (mean +/- 1 SE over M paths), the
    complete-cycle residence average +/- 1 SE (dashed, with band) and the
    analytic cycle mixture (dotted)."""
    A = res["analytic"]
    S = res["sim"]
    X = lambda phi: x0 + phi / (2.0 * math.pi) * w
    Y = lambda v: y0 + h - v / ymax * h

    parts = []
    parts.append(_ln(x0, y0 + h, x0 + w, y0 + h, "ax"))
    parts.append(_ln(x0, y0, x0, y0 + h, "ax"))
    ystep = ymax / 4.0
    v = 0.0
    while v <= ymax + 1e-9:
        parts.append(_ln(x0, Y(v), x0 + w, Y(v), "tick"))
        lab = f"{v:.2f}" if ystep < 0.1 else f"{v:.1f}"
        parts.append(_txt(x0 - 6, Y(v) + 4, lab, "axtext", "end"))
        v += ystep
    for i, s in enumerate(("0", "\u03c0/2", "\u03c0", "3\u03c0/2", "2\u03c0")):
        xx = X(2.0 * math.pi * i / 4.0)
        parts.append(_ln(xx, y0 + h, xx, y0 + h + 4, "ax"))
        parts.append(_txt(xx, y0 + h + 16, s, "axtext"))
    parts.append(_txt(x0 + w / 2, y0 + h + 32, "phase φ", "axtext"))
    yx, yy = x0 - 34, y0 + h / 2
    parts.append(_txt(yx, yy, "Pr{N ≥ θ}", "axtext", "middle",
                      f"rotate(-90 {yx:.1f} {yy:.1f})"))
    parts.append(_txt(x0 + 2, y0 - 10, "(b) phase-resolved Pr{N ≥ θ}",
                      "ptitle", "start"))

    am, ase = S["A_mean"], S["A_se"]
    parts.append(f'<rect x="{x0:.1f}" y="{Y(am + ase):.1f}" width="{w:.1f}" '
                 f'height="{max(1.0, Y(am - ase) - Y(am + ase)):.1f}" '
                 f'class="resband"/>')
    parts.append(_ln(x0, Y(am), x0 + w, Y(am), "resline"))
    parts.append(_ln(x0, Y(A["mixture"]), x0 + w, Y(A["mixture"]), "mixline"))
    parts.append(_poly([(X(p), Y(v)) for p, v in A["p_curve"]], "pcurve"))
    eb = []
    for i in range(res["params"]["n_phases"]):
        xx, m, se = X(A["phases"][i]), S["snap_mean"][i], S["snap_se"][i]
        eb.append(_ln(xx, Y(m + se), xx, Y(m - se), "snapeb"))
        eb.append(_ln(xx - 3, Y(m + se), xx + 3, Y(m + se), "snapeb"))
        eb.append(_ln(xx - 3, Y(m - se), xx + 3, Y(m - se), "snapeb"))
    parts.append("".join(eb))
    parts.append(_dots([(X(A["phases"][i]), Y(S["snap_mean"][i]))
                        for i in range(res["params"]["n_phases"])], "snap"))
    return "".join(parts)


def two_panel_svg(res):
    """Assemble the two-panel figure for one experiment as an SVG string."""
    pmax = max(max(v for _, v in res["analytic"]["p_curve"]),
               res["sim"]["A_mean"] + res["sim"]["A_se"],
               max(res["sim"]["snap_mean"][i] + res["sim"]["snap_se"][i]
                   for i in range(res["params"]["n_phases"])))
    ymax = math.ceil(pmax * 10.0) / 10.0
    parts = ['<svg class="fig" viewBox="0 0 760 320" '
             'xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" role="img" '
             'aria-label="periodic hazard and phase-resolved probability">']
    parts.append(_panel_a(res, 66, 48, 280, 196))
    parts.append(_panel_b(res, 448, 48, 280, 196, ymax))
    parts.append("</svg>")
    return "".join(parts)

What I would argue against / where this breaks

  1. The effect is visible only when the period is long compared with the relaxation time: the relative modulation of mper is ε/√(1+(ω/γ)²), so for ω≫γ the phase curve p(φ) collapses to a constant and the difference between the two estimators falls into the Monte Carlo error. This example needs T=8×(1/γ) to reach a phase dynamics spanning 0.023–0.339. That is a fair criticism; but it is also exactly the regime in which exogenous periods matter most in biology (cell cycle, circadian rhythm, dosing schedule).
  2. The expectation of A equals the mixture by linearity alone; but "converges to the mixture almost surely" is a cycloergodicity-type assumption (the fraction-of-time framework of Gardner et al.). The immigration–death process here is the benign case, not like the core autocatalytic counterexample where long paths replace the ensemble wholesale — so what is shown in this essay is that the two estimators have different targets, not an ergodicity failure.
  3. The Poisson benchmark is exact only when the immigration rate does not depend on N. Any density-dependent b, burst additions or a shared promoter would break the analytic check; for a non-sinusoidal drive (a square wave, say) H is only piecewise smooth at the jumps and needs separate treatment (not tested here). The sampling repair and the estimator distinction themselves are unaffected.
  4. Snapshot samples taken at the same phase in different cycles along one path are correlated. This is controlled by taking a per-path frequency and then the SE over the M paths; with K=40 and 1/γ=1 min ≪ T=8 min the cross-cycle correlation is negligible, but slower relaxation needs clustering by path or a larger K.
  5. Both integrated-hazard root finding and thinning are standard repairs in the literature (Anderson 2007; Lewis–Shedler 1979 as the alternative route), and neither the L4 nor the L5 results are new. What is new is one sentence: under a periodic drive the phase-conditioned ensemble and the cycle mixture are two different estimation targets, and the difference can be resolved numerically.
