Eliminating an RNA lifetime

Author: Yan Runzi · Week 3 (2026-09-20) · CCBS 2026

If we average out a fast promoter state, Lecture 6 §9a replaces a two-state burst generator by a briefer, sharper Poisson approximation — and whether bursts survive depends on which rate is fixed. This problem repeats that whole argument with the hidden clock moved one level down: instead of a promoter, we eliminate an RNA lifetime. An RNA that translates and is destroyed by two independent exponential clocks leaves behind, before it dies, a count of proteins whose law we can write down exactly, with no master equation at all.

The assignment · what to hand in

Let an RNA translate at rate kp and disappear at γm. Use two competing clocks to derive its geometric protein packet, with its mean, variance, and Fano factor. Then decide (a) what survives if both rates increase at fixed ratio, and (b) what survives if translation stays fixed. This essay state–event–probability structure follows Lectures 5–6.

Part I

The claim, in two clocks

The packet law is a race between translation and degradation.

1One transcript, two clocks

A single RNA is the whole object; its production of protein is the whole observable.

A single transcript M is born at time zero. While it lives it translates — each event deposits one protein with rate kp — and it is destroyed at rate γm. Nothing is hidden and nothing is coarse-grained beyond Lectures 3 and 5: these are composite channels with declared clocks, the squiggle-arrow language of the course, fully reduced to a propensity table. The transcript is the only tiny number in the system — a protein count P is large, so it is the one quantity a population measurement can actually average.

The object

Only two clocks exist: translation at kp, degradation at γm. They race, the transcript remembers nothing between rounds, and the whole derivation below is one pass over a single ladder — no master equation, no Gillespie.

2A race gives a geometric packet

Whichever exponential clock wins first decides both terms of a geometric law.

Each event that happens to the live RNA is a draw from a race. Translation wins a single race with probability q, after which the race restarts with unchanged clocks — the transcript is memoryless, exactly because both waiting times are exponential. The count of translation wins before the first degradation is therefore a sequence of independent races terminated by the degradation race.

(1) q = kp/(kp + γm),     1 − q = γm/(kp + γm)

Condition on surviving j consecutive races — that is qj — then end with one degradation race, 1 − q. Multiply and the packet is geometric: the lecture figure's count ladder read on its side.

(2) Pr(B = j) = (1 − q)·qj,     j = 0, 1, 2, …
(3) E[B] = q/(1 − q) = kp/γm,     Var(B) = B̄(1 + B̄)

The two exponential clocks tie only with probability zero, so every race ends, and Σj (1 − q)qj = 1 exactly: a finite packet is guaranteed, degradation is reached almost surely, and no probability mass is ever lost. This is Lecture 5's burst addendum (B̄ = ktl/dm) written as a race, and the same math as Lecture 6 §9a's "an ON period produces a geometric packet of mean ρ/β."

Definition (geometric packet)

The protein packet B left by one RNA is geometric, Pr(B = j) = (1 − q)qj with q = kp/(kp + γm). Its mean and variance pin the two things a burst contributes: the level kp/γm and the excess noise B̄(1 + B̄).

Part II

The two limits

Speed nothing up in isolation — hold the mean, change the noise.

Both parts of the assignment keep mean protein production kp·⟨M⟩ fixed, by letting the transcript source rm compensate. The two limits differ in which clock is left to set the noise; the mean is a red herring that cannot tell them apart.

3Both rates up at fixed ratio: the packet survives

Because E[B] is a function of the ratio alone, the whole geometric law is invariant.

Scale the two clocks together, kp, γm → ∞ with kp/γm held fixed. Then q in (1) never moves, so the entire law (2) — every probability, the mean, the width, and the excess Fano — is unchanged. What changes is only the tempo: each packet is completed in a briefer real time, so packets arrive faster but carry the same fixed size.

Survival

In the limit kp, γm → ∞ at fixed ratio, the geometric packet survives as a burst process: Poisson-arriving packets of fixed mean, presentable without resolving any transcript. This is Lecture 6 §9a's burst limit, and it gives the protein Fano F = 1 + B̄.

What is eliminated is every temporal feature of a transcript: the lifetime 1/γm and the finite width of a burst episode are pressed toward an instant. The transcript's history is averaged out; its size is not. This is the crucial asymmetry of the burst-process construction of Lecture 5 §3.

4Translation fixed: packets dissolve into Poisson

As γm→∞ at fixed kp, the mean packet collapses and the excess noise with it.

Now kill the transcript faster, γm → ∞ with kp held fixed, and let the source rise to keep kp·⟨M⟩ constant — more transcripts, each shorter-lived. Now E[B] = kp/γm → 0.

Survival

The geometric packet degenerates: it becomes zero (almost always) or one (rarely). Compensating transcripts keep protein flowing, but almost every protein is now its own lone event, so arrivals become Poisson and the protein Fano falls to F = 1. What survives is the mean production rate kp·⟨M⟩ and the elementary translation event; what is eliminated is the packet and its excess noise. This is Lecture 6 §9a's fast-fixed-rate-switching → Fano-one limit.

Because both limits share every mean, they are indistinguishable by a mean measurement alone. Only a second-moment observable — the Fano factor or a waiting-time statistic — can tell whether the surviving signal is a burst process or a Poisson rain. That is the lesson of Lecture 6 §9a in miniature, and the reason this problem is a Fano-factor problem at heart.

Part III

Consequences

One number the derivation actually produced.

5A packet with a Fano of eleven

At reference rates the packet's excess noise dwarfs anything a mean tells you.

Take a convenient reference: translation kp = 2 proteins/min, degradation γm = 0.2/min. Then the transcript lives 1/γm = 5 minutes, and each packet carries, on average, ten proteins. Averaging is exactly what obscures the point.

What the numbers give

At kp = 2, γm = 0.2, the packet B is geometric with mean B̄ = 10, variance B̄(1 + B̄) = 110, and coefficient of variation √(110/100) ≈ 1.05. Closing the transcript loop (fast-mRNA limit, §9a) gives a protein Fano factor F = 1 + B̄ = 11 — an eleven-fold over-dispersion. In Limit A that number is frozen; in Limit B it collapses toward 1. A measured Fano below 1 stands apart from every row of this model.

Three views, three operations. Packet law — exact, from (1)–(2), no simulation needed. Mean & Fano — analytic, E[B] = kp/γm and F = 1 + B̄ in the fast-mRNA limit. Part IV

Situating and testing

Where this sits in the lectures, and how it would fail.

6Where this could be wrong

An observable that would refute the model, so someone could go and make it.

The two limits agree on every mean, so the honest way to close the problem is with the second-moment observable, as Lecture 6 §9a leaves it. The footer estimates are the falsifiers:

  • Sub-Poisson protein count. A measured Fano plainly below 1, at fixed fast-mRNA rates, rejects this model outright — no choice of kp or γm permits it. This is the cleanest kill.
  • Fano above 1 + B̄. A larger excess noise rejects the geometric-packet assumption itself — it points to real promoter-level bursts (Lecture 6's two-state generator), not an RNA-lifetime race. The packet law (2) names its own upper bound on Fano.
  • Least-informative measurement. Any experiment that records only mean protein level is exactly the wrong tool here: by construction it cannot separate Limit A from Limit B. The model's value is precisely that it continues to distinguish them when the mean cannot.
Open

The observation that would settle it: a single-cell assay of protein-vs-mRNA noise as transcript stability is titrated. If a fixed mRNA lifetime can be made short while keeping kp constant, the Fano should fall from 1 + B̄ toward 1 — the packets dissolving in real time. If instead the measured over-dispersion stubbornly holds while γm rises, the surplus noise must be living above the transcript level, and this week's model is missing a clock.
