1 · We already know the fast and slow clocks

Half-substrate-time calculation

From −Ṡ=VmaxS/(KM+S), separate variables and integrate: Vmaxt=S₀−S+KMln(S₀/S). At S=S₀/2, S₀=KM=1 μM and Vmax=0.05 μM/s, t=(0.5+ln2)/0.05=23.8629 s. This is a prediction of the reduced model, not a measured time or an exact full-model result.

Exact nondimensionalization

Let A=KM+S₀, C*₀=ETS₀/A, τC=1/(k₁A), τS=A/(k₂ET). Set x=C/C*₀, y=S/S₀, T=t/τS, ε=τC/τS, η=ET/A, α=S₀/A, β=KM/A and λ=k₁A/k₂=η/ε. Then the full model is exactly

εx′=f(x,y)=y−(αy+β)x,
y′=−x−λf(x,y).

The second equation follows from the exact identity y′+ηx′=−x. For our example: α=β=1/2, λ=4, η=0.05 and ε=0.0125. Small ε is taken by ET→0 at fixed rate constants and S₀. Then λ stays fixed and η→0 as well. Simply taking k₂→0 is a different limiting path.

On the slow clock, f=0 gives x=y/(αy+β) and y′=−x. On the fast clock θ=T/ε, dx/dθ=f and dy/dθ=ε(−x−λf)→0. The fast derivative ∂f/∂x=−(αy+β) is strictly negative for y≥0, so the frozen fast balance is attracting. Initial-layer matching and error bounds still require a specified region and time window.

A moving quasi-steady target

h(S)=ETS/(KM+S) has h′(S)=ETKM/(KM+S)²>0. As substrate decreases, the target h(S) decreases. The approximation C≈h(S) therefore gives Ċ≈h′(S)Ṡ, not a permanently frozen complex.

The full finite-parameter trajectory is not exactly on the C-nullcline. A small deviation supplies the residual net rate needed for slow motion. The critical limiting curve, the finite-parameter nullcline and an invariant slow manifold are distinct concepts; a moving-target approximation describes the leading slow behavior.

Freezing S during initial complex formation is supported by a suitable reactant-stationary/small-enzyme regime, for example η=ET/(KM+S₀)≪1 with negligible initial complex. The clock ratio alone is not a universal sufficient condition across arbitrary parameter paths.
