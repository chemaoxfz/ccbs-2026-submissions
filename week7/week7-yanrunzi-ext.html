From scoring poses to generating backbones

What machine learning changed in the design pipeline for protein binders

Author: Runzi Yan · Week 7 · CCBS 2026

A binder is successful only if it folds, recognizes the intended target surface and produces the required effect. Lecture 15 distinguishes sequence, structure and behavior; I want to push that distinction one step further. Machine learning's major contribution is a change in where the design pipeline gets its structural proposals. Rosetta-guided design searches candidates using explicit physical scores, whereas generative models can propose new backbones directly from structural constraints.

1Rosetta: search a designed structural space

Cao and colleagues' 2020 study aimed to design miniproteins that bind the SARS-CoV-2 Spike receptor-binding domain (RBD) and compete with ACE2. It used two Rosetta-guided routes: Blueprint Builder incorporated the ACE2 α-helix into a supporting scaffold (the AHB route), while a de novo route docked a library of small helical scaffolds against the RBD using rotamer interaction fields (RIF). LCB1 came from this second route, not from the ACE2-helix design. RIF organizes favorable side-chain rotamer contacts so candidate poses can be searched for shape and chemical complementarity; sequences were then optimized and experimentally screened.1

Here the search is guided by approximate physical terms for packing, hydrogen bonds, solvation and geometry. But its result depends on which scaffolds were generated, which poses were sampled and how the score represents the interface. The central question is: which candidate in this chosen structural space scores best?

2AI: generate a backbone, then design its sequence

RFdiffusion changes the source of that structural space. Fine-tuned from RoseTTAFold for structure denoising, it generates backbones under constraints such as a target surface, a preserved motif or symmetry. Instead of searching only a pre-built library, it proposes new geometries compatible with the task. In binder design, these backbones describe candidate frameworks at the target interface.2

ProteinMPNN takes a proposed backbone and solves a complementary problem: it uses local structural context to design compatible amino-acid sequences. RFdiffusion asks what backbone could satisfy these constraints?; ProteinMPNN asks what sequence could fit this backbone?3 Separating these stages allows many sequences to be sampled for a generated backbone, and multiple backbones to be explored for one target.

Structure prediction then checks consistency. Lecture 15's RFdiffusion workflow uses AlphaFold2 to test whether a designed sequence returns a compatible fold. For a binder, AlphaFold3 can additionally predict the joint binder–target complex, including protein–protein interactions. AF3 is a complex predictor, not a sequence generator, and a plausible predicted interface is not a binding measurement.4

3The progress is real; function still has to be measured

The advance is not that machine learning made Rosetta obsolete: LCB1 shows that physics-guided design can produce a potent, experimentally characterized binder. The change is methodological. A designer can condition a generative model on a target geometry, explore new backbone architectures, design sequences with a separate inverse-folding model, and use complex prediction to prioritize candidates. This widens the proposal stage beyond manually enumerated scaffold topologies.

But the output is still a candidate. A predicted complex cannot establish expression, solubility, specificity or inhibition. Those require experiments: purification, a direct binding assay with negative controls, and a functional assay of the intended effect. AI has expanded the search; experiments still decide whether a binder works.

The point pushed beyond Lecture 15 The most important shift is from score-guided search within a chosen structural space to constraint-guided generation of new structural proposals. Sequence design and structure prediction then make those proposals testable—but only experiments can turn a predicted binder into a validated one.
