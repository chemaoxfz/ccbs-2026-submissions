When is extra energy worth its resource and damage cost?

Proteome allocation, target function and a Pareto boundary

CCBS · EST 5053 · Lectures 13–14 · Week 7 · 2026-10-26

The claim. The design target is not maximum source flux but the Pareto relation between target function and cell costs. If output saturates while expression burden or damage rises, the optimum can be intermediate or zero expression. Active, abundance-matched and route-defective controls separate function from cost.

1 · Problem and scope

An energy module consumes protein, membrane, substrate and maintenance capacity. This coarse model uses one source and one consumer; it is not a whole-cell reconstruction and does not make growth the universal objective.

2 · Resource–function model

φ_aux+φ_native+φ_growth≤1
J_source=min(J_input,k_cat,aux E_aux,J_acceptor,J_membrane)
J_target=min(η_route J_source,V_target,J_substrate)
B=B_max J_target/(K_B+J_target)
N=z(B)−w_growth z(μ_ref−μ)−w_damage z(D)−w_expr z(φ_aux).

The primary output is the Pareto set (B, μ, D, φ_aux); the scalar N is a declared engineering preference, not a natural constant.

3 · Assay and controls

Titrate measured module abundance over at least five levels, including no expression and a high-expression stress point, at fixed external input. Measure abundance, source flux, target output, growth, viability, substrate/acceptor use, membrane/pH/ROS damage and native consumer amount.

Use at least four independent biological replicates for a final Pareto estimate. Fit hierarchical/segmented models and bootstrap at the biological-replicate level. Report per-cell and per-target-protein output separately; select scalar weights before seeing results.

4 · Illustrative result

For φ_aux∈[0,0.65], let J_target=4φ/(0.5+4φ), growth=(0.65−φ)/0.65 and damage=0.8φ². With N=J_target−0.5(1−growth)−0.8φ², the synthetic maximum is near φ_aux=0.211: J_target=0.628, growth=0.675, N=0.430.

5 · Falsification and limits

Prediction. If active expression raises source flux but never beats the matched inactive/burden control in target output, no net source–consumer benefit is demonstrated. If growth and damage never change, the penalty is unsupported or unmeasured. An optimum at the upper boundary requires extending the titration.

Proteome, membrane and molecule fractions are different currencies. FBA gives feasible fluxes, not the biological objective. Growth is a cost, not a universal target. Correlated burden, leakage, ROS and substrate depletion need orthogonal readouts; population Pareto fronts can hide bet-hedging.
