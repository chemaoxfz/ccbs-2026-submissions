Week 8 — Can one binding time course give both kon and koff?

Xinyu Wu · Week 8 (lectures 15–16) · CCBS 2026

One sentence: when the signal scale is unknown, a binding time course at a single ligand concentration fixes only kobs = k+S + k−, so separating k+ from k− needs at least two concentrations, and k− is estimated best when the two bracket Kd about six-fold apart rather than as far apart as possible.

The question

Lecture 15 closes with a reversible binding reaction: two pairs of rate constants with the same Kd = 1 µM respond with time constants of 0.5 s and 5 s, so a requirement such as “reach 90% of equilibrium occupancy within two seconds” needs a time-resolved binding measurement [1]. Lecture 16 names parameter unidentifiability as one reason current whole-cell models do not compose [2]. Put together, they raise an experimental question: after a binder has been designed, which time courses must be measured so that k+ and k− can each be handed to a cell model?

The model

I keep the Lecture 15 setting: free ligand S clamped by a reservoir, independent sites, protein initially unbound [1]. The occupied fraction is

θ(t) = A (1 − e−kobs t),  A = k+S / kobs,  kobs = k+S + k−.

An instrument reports a signal y = F·θ, for example fluorescence, where F is the signal of fully bound protein and is usually unknown. Demonstration values: k+ = 1 µM−1 s−1 and k− = 1 s−1 (the “fast pair” of Lecture 15, Kd = 1 µM), F = 1, Gaussian noise with standard deviation σ = 0.02, and 120 time points per experimental design, log-spaced from 0.01 to 10 s and split equally between concentrations. None of these are measured values.

One concentration: two observables, three unknowns

A single curve y(t) = F·A (1 − e−kobs t) is set by two numbers, its plateau F·A and its rate kobs. The unknowns are F, k+ and k−, so every point on the line k+S + k− = kobs fits equally well once F is adjusted. Figure 1a shows one case. At 1 µM, (k+, k−, F) = (1, 1, 1) and (0.5, 1.5, 2) give identical curves (maximum difference 0), yet the second set has Kd = 3 µM. At 4 µM their plateaus are 0.80 and 1.14; the difference of 0.34 is about 17 times the noise.

If F is known, so that the signal is calibrated to occupancy, one concentration suffices: the relative standard errors of k+ and k− are 2.1% and 2.9%. Calibrating F, however, requires a measurement near saturation, which amounts to a second concentration.

Two concentrations: a slope and an intercept

kobs is linear in S, with slope k+ and intercept k−. Two concentrations give two rates and two plateaus, four numbers for three unknowns, and the parameters become identifiable. How well depends on how far apart the two points are.

DesignRelative s.e. of k+Relative s.e. of k−Correlation of the two estimates
1 µM, F unknownnot identifiable
1 µM, F known2.1%2.9%0.89
0.8 + 1.25 µM6.3%6.7%−0.73
0.5 + 2 µM2.8%3.8%−0.05
0.2 + 5 µM2.0%4.4%0.03

Table 1. Fisher-information estimates. Each identifiable design was checked against 1000 refits of noisy simulated data; the standard errors agree within 3% of each other.

The error in k+ falls steadily as the concentrations move apart, because a longer baseline fixes the slope. The error in k− does not: it is smallest, 3.7%, at a ratio of about 6.3 (0.40 and 2.52 µM) and grows again beyond that. At the low end the plateau A = S/(S + Kd) shrinks into the noise; at the high end kobs ≈ k+S, and k− becomes a small intercept reached only by extrapolation. For k−, the two concentrations should bracket Kd rather than be pushed as far apart as possible.

This changes what a design report should contain. The two-second requirement of Lecture 15 needs only kobs at the working concentration, which one curve answers. A cell model needs k+ and k− separately, because ligand concentration in a cell is not fixed. A binder delivered as a component should therefore come with time courses at two or more concentrations around Kd, or with a calibrated single curve together with the saturating measurement used to calibrate it.

Where this breaks

The result inherits the simplifications of the Lecture 15 model: clamped ligand, independent sites, a single-exponential response and noise that does not scale with the signal. The optimal ratio of 6.3 depends on the time window, the number of points and the noise level, and must be recomputed for other choices. Bracketing Kd also presupposes a rough Kd estimate before the kinetic experiment. Ligand depletion, multistep binding or a conformational change after binding make the response deviate from one exponential and need a different model; in those cases two concentrations may not be enough.

Code

binding_identifiability.py — identifiability, Fisher information, Monte Carlo check and ratio scan
"""Week 8: which binding time courses separate k_on from k_off?

Model (Lecture 15, free ligand clamped, independent sites, start unbound):
    theta(t) = S/(S+Kd) * (1 - exp(-kobs t)),  kobs = kp*S + km,  Kd = km/kp
Observed signal: y = F*theta + noise, F = unknown signal scale (e.g. fluorescence per
fully bound site). All parameter values are demonstration values, not measurements.
"""
import json
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import least_squares

KP, KM, F = 1.0, 1.0, 1.0      # uM^-1 s^-1, s^-1, signal units (L15 "fast pair")
SIGMA = 0.02                    # noise s.d., same units as F (demo)
N_TOTAL = 120                   # total time points in every design
T = np.geomspace(0.01, 10.0, 60)  # s; each concentration gets 60 points

def curve(t, S, kp, km, f):
    kobs = kp * S + km
    return f * kp * S / kobs * (1 - np.exp(-kobs * t))

def design_points(concs):
    n_each = N_TOTAL // len(concs)
    t = np.geomspace(0.01, 10.0, n_each)
    return [(S, t) for S in concs]

def model(params, pts, f_known):
    kp, km = np.exp(params[:2])
    f = F if f_known else np.exp(params[2])
    return np.concatenate([curve(t, S, kp, km, f) for S, t in pts])

def fisher_se(pts, f_known):
    """Standard errors of ln kp, ln km (relative errors) from J^T J / sigma^2."""
    p0 = np.log([KP, KM] + ([] if f_known else [F]))
    h = 1e-6
    J = np.column_stack([(model(p0 + h * e, pts, f_known) - model(p0 - h * e, pts, f_known)) / (2 * h)
                         for e in np.eye(len(p0))])
    M = J.T @ J / SIGMA**2
    cond = np.linalg.cond(M)
    if cond > 1e12:
        return None, cond
    C = np.linalg.inv(M)
    return C[:2, :2], cond

def monte_carlo(pts, f_known, n=1000, seed=8):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    p_true = np.log([KP, KM] + ([] if f_known else [F]))
    y0 = model(p_true, pts, f_known)
    est = []
    for _ in range(n):
        y = y0 + rng.normal(0, SIGMA, y0.size)
        r = least_squares(lambda p: model(p, pts, f_known) - y, p_true + 0.3)
        est.append(r.x[:2])
    est = np.array(est)
    return est.std(axis=0), np.corrcoef(est.T)[0, 1]

designs = {
    "1 uM, calibrated F": ([1.0], True),
    "1 uM, unknown F": ([1.0], False),
    "0.8 + 1.25 uM": ([0.8, 1.25], False),
    "0.5 + 2 uM": ([0.5, 2.0], False),
    "0.2 + 5 uM": ([0.2, 5.0], False),
}

results = {}
for name, (concs, f_known) in designs.items():
    pts = design_points(concs)
    C, cond = fisher_se(pts, f_known)
    row = {"concs_uM": concs, "F_known": f_known, "fisher_condition": float(cond)}
    if C is None:
        row["identifiable"] = False
    else:
        se = np.sqrt(np.diag(C))
        mc_se, mc_r = monte_carlo(pts, f_known)
        row.update(identifiable=True,
                   cv_kp_fisher=float(se[0]), cv_km_fisher=float(se[1]),
                   corr_fisher=float(C[0, 1] / se[0] / se[1]),
                   cv_kp_mc=float(mc_se[0]), cv_km_mc=float(mc_se[1]), corr_mc=float(mc_r),
                   cov_ln=C.tolist())
    results[name] = row

# Exact degeneracy at a single concentration with unknown F:
# any (kp', km') with kp'S + km' = kobs reproduces the curve if F' = F*kp/kp'.
S = 1.0
alt = (0.5, 1.5)          # kp', km'  -> same kobs = 2 s^-1, Kd' = 3 uM
f_alt = F * KP / alt[0]   # = 2
t = np.linspace(0, 5, 501)
dev_1 = np.max(np.abs(curve(t, S, KP, KM, F) - curve(t, S, *alt, f_alt)))
dev_4 = np.max(np.abs(curve(t, 4.0, KP, KM, F) - curve(t, 4.0, *alt, f_alt)))
results["degeneracy"] = {"alt_kp": alt[0], "alt_km": alt[1], "alt_F": f_alt,
                         "max_diff_at_1uM": float(dev_1), "max_diff_at_4uM": float(dev_4),
                         "plateau_true_4uM": float(curve(1e3, 4.0, KP, KM, F)),
                         "plateau_alt_4uM": float(curve(1e3, 4.0, *alt, f_alt))}

json.dump(results, open("week8-results.json", "w"), indent=2)
for k, v in results.items():
    print(k, {a: (round(b, 4) if isinstance(b, float) else b) for a, b in v.items() if a != "cov_ln"})

# Scan the spread of two concentrations around a geometric mean of 1 uM (= Kd).
ratios = np.geomspace(1.05, 400, 120)
scan = []
for r in ratios:
    C, _ = fisher_se(design_points([1 / np.sqrt(r), np.sqrt(r)]), False)
    se = np.sqrt(np.diag(C))
    scan.append((r, se[0], se[1]))
scan = np.array(scan)
i_best = np.argmin(scan[:, 2])   # column 2 = CV of k_off
print("km CV minimum at ratio %.1f (S = %.2f, %.2f uM): cv_kp %.4f cv_km %.4f"
      % (scan[i_best, 0], 1 / np.sqrt(scan[i_best, 0]), np.sqrt(scan[i_best, 0]), scan[i_best, 1], scan[i_best, 2]))
results["scan_best_km"] = {"ratio": float(scan[i_best, 0]), "cv_kp": float(scan[i_best, 1]), "cv_km": float(scan[i_best, 2])}
np.savetxt("ratio-scan.csv", scan, delimiter=",", header="ratio,cv_kp,cv_km", comments="")
json.dump(results, open("week8-results.json", "w"), indent=2)
