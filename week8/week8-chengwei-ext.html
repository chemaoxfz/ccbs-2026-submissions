Why binding alone does not make a protein switch

Chengwei Jin · Week 8 · CCBS 2026 · 3 November 2026

One sentence: In a two-state equilibrium model, binding changes the balance of shapes only if the ligand has different affinities for the two states.

The point I am adding

The guest lecture presents sequence design for a target structure and a binder-design workflow.1 I ask a further question: what makes ligand binding drive a protein into a different shape? The design challenge is to connect recognition of an input to redistribution between conformations.

Work it

Praetorius and colleagues designed hinge proteins with two structured conformations, X and Y, making Y capable of binding a peptide.2 For hinge cs207, they used DEER spectroscopy to estimate the distance distribution between two attached spin labels. I digitized the experimental peak positions in Figure 2C. The dominant distance shifts from approximately 40 Å without peptide to 29 Å with excess peptide, a shortening of about 11 Å. This supplies experimental evidence of a conformational response.

A minimal equilibrium model isolates why preference matters. Let R(c) be the total Y-to-X population ratio at free ligand concentration c, counting both bound and unbound proteins. Let KX and KY be the dissociation constants for ligand binding to X and Y. A smaller constant means stronger binding. Assume one binding site per conformation. For either state, its bound population is c/K times its unbound population. Adding both populations gives the factor 1 + c/K. The unbound Y-to-X ratio remains R(0), its ligand-free value, so

R(c)R(0)=1+c/KY1+c/KX.

If KX = KY, the factors cancel: binding leaves the state ratio unchanged, even when many proteins bind ligand. If KY < KX, ligand increases the relative population of Y. Thus, in this model, a ligand must prefer one conformation to drive redistribution. The equal-affinity comparison is a model prediction, not a control experiment in the paper.

What I would argue against / where this breaks

The distance shift does not determine the two state-specific affinities or how quickly switching occurs. Unequal affinities alone also do not guarantee a large response: the initial state balance and ligand concentration matter. Designing a switch therefore requires coupling ligand recognition to a change in conformational preference, beyond designing two structures that can exist.

Reproduce the figure and numbers · Python, no extra packages

Save the code as reproduce.py and run python reproduce.py. It converts documented pixel coordinates to distances and writes the SVG, CSV and JSON. The coordinates come from the original embedded PDF images. Solid-curve apices were selected by a color mask within isolated peak regions; dashed design predictions were excluded. This reproduces the digitized peak summary, not a reanalysis of raw DEER traces.

"""Reproduce the experimental-distance figure with standard-library Python 3.

Source: Praetorius et al. (2023), Science, doi:10.1126/science.adg7731,
Figure 2C, hinge cs207, label-site pair 1. Solid curves without/with peptide.
Original PDF raster calibrated against three shared axis ticks. A color mask
located each solid-curve apex in an isolated region. These are digitized modes
of experimental-derived distributions, not raw DEER traces or state fractions.
Round to whole angstroms. No error bars inferred. The project also includes
analysis/digitize.py to repeat extraction from the source PDF.

Run: python reproduce.py
Outputs: figures/conformational-shift.svg, modal-distances.csv, results.json.
"""
import csv
import json
from pathlib import Path

AXIS_TICKS=[(115.0,20.0),(348.0,40.0),(581.0,60.0)]  # pixel x, angstroms
PEAK_PIXELS=[('No peptide',345.5),('+ peptide',220.5)]

def results():
    mx=sum(p[0] for p in AXIS_TICKS)/3
    my=sum(p[1] for p in AXIS_TICKS)/3
    slope=sum((x-mx)*(y-my) for x,y in AXIS_TICKS)/sum((x-mx)**2 for x,y in AXIS_TICKS)
    intercept=my-slope*mx
    rows=[dict(condition=name,peak_pixel=x,mode_A=slope*x+intercept,
               rounded_mode_A=round(slope*x+intercept)) for name,x in PEAK_PIXELS]
    return dict(modes=rows,shift_A=rows[0]['mode_A']-rows[1]['mode_A'],
                slope_A_per_pixel=slope,intercept_A=intercept)

def make_svg(data):
    out=['<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 560 280" role="img" aria-labelledby="shift-title shift-desc">',
         '<title id="shift-title">The same hinge changes conformation when peptide is added</title>',
         '<desc id="shift-desc">cs207, spin-label pair 1. Digitized modal distance is about 40 angstroms without peptide and 29 with peptide, about 11 angstroms shorter.</desc>',
         '<rect width="560" height="280" rx="8" fill="var(--soft,#f6f8fa)"/>',
         '<g font-family="Arial,Helvetica,sans-serif" fill="var(--ink,#20201E)">',
         '<text x="20" y="30" text-anchor="start" font-size="17">cs207 · same protein, same label sites</text>']
    for i,row in enumerate(data['modes']):
        y=95+i*76
        x=150+(row['mode_A']-20)*9
        color=['var(--peri,#5A73CE)','var(--coral,#EE8168)'][i]
        out += [f'<line x1="150" y1="{y}" x2="510" y2="{y}" stroke="var(--line,#d8dee4)"/>',
                f'<text x="20" y="{y+6}" text-anchor="start" font-size="18">{row["condition"]}</text>',
                f'<line x1="{x:.6f}" y1="{y+8}" x2="{x:.6f}" y2="221" stroke="var(--muted,#566)" stroke-dasharray="3 5"/>',
                f'<circle cx="{x:.6f}" cy="{y}" r="7" fill="{color}"/>',
                f'<text x="{x+13:.6f}" y="{y-14}" text-anchor="start" font-size="18">≈ {row["rounded_mode_A"]} Å</text>']
    out += ['<line x1="150" y1="221" x2="510" y2="221" stroke="var(--ink,#20201E)"/>']
    for tick in range(20,61,10):
        x=150+(tick-20)*9
        out += [f'<line x1="{x}" y1="221" x2="{x}" y2="226" stroke="var(--ink,#20201E)"/>',
                f'<text x="{x}" y="244" text-anchor="middle" font-size="14">{tick}</text>']
    out += ['<text x="330" y="270" text-anchor="middle" font-size="15">Most likely spin-label distance (Å)</text>','</g></svg>']
    return '\n'.join(out)

def main():
    here=Path(__file__).resolve().parent
    root=here.parent if here.name in ('analysis','qa') else here
    out=root/'figures';out.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
    data=results()
    (out/'conformational-shift.svg').write_text(make_svg(data),encoding='utf-8')
    (out/'results.json').write_text(json.dumps(data,indent=2),encoding='utf-8')
    with (out/'modal-distances.csv').open('w',newline='',encoding='utf-8') as f:
        w=csv.DictWriter(f,fieldnames=list(data['modes'][0]));w.writeheader();w.writerows(data['modes'])
    for row in data['modes']:print(f'{row["condition"]}: {row["mode_A"]:.3f} A, reported ~{row["rounded_mode_A"]} A')
    print(f'Shortening: {data["shift_A"]:.3f} A, reported ~{round(data["shift_A"])} A')

if __name__=='__main__':main()
