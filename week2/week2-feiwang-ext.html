Week 2 Extension: Malignant Transformation Rewrites Gene-Expression Parameters

Author: fei wang · Week 2 · CCBS 2026

One sentence: When a normal pancreatic cell becomes a pancreatic cancer cell, the production-minus-removal structure of gene expression remains intact, but transcription, translation, RNA and protein lifetimes, growth dilution, gene dosage, and protein activity can all change—so the same observed protein increase can arise from different hidden mechanisms.

The point I am adding

Lecture 3 resolves a lumped gene-expression arrow into two dynamical pools: messenger RNA M and protein P.1 I use that same accounting to ask what changes when a normal pancreatic epithelial cell moves toward pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). The answer is not that cancer invents a new balance law. Production is still opposed by removal. What changes are the regulatory function and the parameters inside it.

The extension matters because the phrase “gene X is upregulated in cancer” is mechanistically incomplete. More protein can come from stronger transcription, faster translation, slower mRNA decay, slower protein decay, altered dilution by growth, increased gene copy number, or a change in protein activity that occurs even without a large abundance change. This is a theoretical transfer of the lecture model, with illustrative parameters rather than a fitted tumor dataset.

Work it

Let R denote the effective transcriptional regulator state, M the mRNA of a gene of interest, and P its protein product. Let qD denote gene/promoter dosage, C an effective chromatin state, and μ a mutation or protein-state variable. Normal and tumor cells share the same bookkeeping:

Normal:   dMN/dt = ftxN(RN,qDN,CN) − (δMN + λN)MN dPN/dt = ktlNMN − (δPN + λN)PN Tumor:   dMT/dt = ftxT(RT,qDT,CT) − (δMT + λT)MT dPT/dt = ktlTMT − (δPT + λT)PT

The structure does not change: mRNA is produced and removed; protein is produced from mRNA and removed by intrinsic turnover plus dilution. The tumor state is therefore a different parameter set, not a different grammar. Integrated genomic studies of PDAC show recurrent alterations in KRAS, TP53, CDKN2A, SMAD4, and other regulators, consistent with the idea that the regulatory inputs and gene-state landscape are rewired during progression.2

At steady state, the two pools solve in causal order:

M* = ftx(R,qD,C)δM + λ P* = ktlδP + λM* = ktl ftx(R,qD,C)(δM+λ)(δP+λ)

So the tumor-to-normal protein fold change decomposes into separate mechanistic contributions:

PT*PN* = ktlTktlN × ftxTftxN × δMN+λNδMT+λT × δPN+λNδPT+λT.

This identity is the useful part of the extension. A two-fold protein increase does not tell us which factor supplied the two-fold change. It is a phenotype that several mechanisms can produce.

Protein abundance is still not the final state variable. An oncogenic mutation can change function without a proportionate abundance change. Introduce a functional activity A:

A = h(P, μ, Φ, L) μ: mutation state · Φ: post-translational state · L: localization / interaction state

KRAS makes the distinction concrete. Oncogenic KRAS can drive persistent downstream signaling and pancreatic tumorigenesis because the biochemical state of the protein is altered; the relevant change is not simply “more KRAS protein.”3 In the reduced model, it is therefore possible to have PT ≈ PN while AT ≫ AN.

Step-by-step derivation and assumptions

The normal and tumor equations both follow the same addition-minus-removal accounting. Setting dM/dt = 0 gives M* = ftx/(δM+λ). Substituting that result into dP/dt = 0 gives P* = ktlftx/[(δM+λ)(δP+λ)]. Dividing the tumor expression by the normal expression produces the fold-change factorization above.

If the promoter source is itself resolved, a repressor-controlled transcription law can be written schematically as ftx(R) = r0 + (r1−r0)Kn/(Kn+Rn). In cancer, R, K, n, r0, r1, and qD are effective quantities that can shift because transcription-factor activity, chromatin accessibility, enhancer state, or copy number has changed.

The steady-state ratio does not by itself identify which term changed. Distinguishing transcription from RNA stabilization, translation, and protein stabilization requires separate measurements. Recent PDAC literature also emphasizes post-transcriptional control of mRNA stability as a contributor to malignant adaptation.4

What I would argue against / where this breaks

An increased tumor protein level is not proof of increased transcription. Unchanged mRNA is not proof that the pathway is unchanged, and unchanged protein abundance is not proof of unchanged activity. The reduced model also treats each species as one well-mixed pool and compresses promoter occupancy, chromatin, translation machinery, degradation systems, spatial compartmentalization, and feedback into effective parameters.

The steady-state formulas require a parameter regime to persist long enough for relaxation. During real pancreatic carcinogenesis, the parameters themselves evolve as cells move through precursor and malignant states. A more general description is therefore Θ(t) = {R(t), qD(t), C(t), ktl(t), δM(t), δP(t), λ(t), μ(t)}. A tumor is also multicellular, so these effective parameters can differ across epithelial cells, fibroblasts, immune cells, and spatial microenvironments. The model is useful because it separates hypotheses; it is not a claim that one universal PDAC rate constant exists.
