How much space does the E. coli genome occupy?

A 1.56 mm molecule inside a micrometre-scale cell sounds impossible, until length is separated from volume.

Extension mini-essay · Your Name · Lecture 2 · CCBS 2026

1The claim

Lecture 2 introduced the proteins and genome inside a cell. Escherichia coli contains about 4.6 million base pairs of DNA.1 How can such a large genome be organized inside one tiny cell? Before asking how the chromosome is folded, I focus on a simpler geometric question: how much of the cell volume would one genome occupy as bare DNA?

My claim is that the genome poses a severe organization problem, but not a material-capacity problem: bare DNA occupies only about 0.5% of a typical E. coli cell.

2Four numbers

Everything in the estimate follows from four sourced, order-of-magnitude inputs.

Table 1. Inputs for one E. coli genome equivalent.
QuantitySymbolValueSource
Genome sizeN4.6 × 106 bp[1]
Axial rise per base paird0.34 nm/bp[2]
Bare-DNA radiusr≈ 1 nm[2]
Typical cell volumeVcell≈ 1 fL[3]

3The estimate

First multiply genome size by the spacing between base pairs. A fully extended chromosome has contour length

L = Nd = (4.6 × 106 bp)(0.34 nm/bp) = 1.56 mm.

Now treat the double helix as a solid cylinder. Because 1 fL = 109 nm3,

VDNA = πr2L = 4.9 × 10−3 fL,   so   VDNA/Vcell = 0.49%.

The two results are not contradictory. DNA can be extremely long and still use little material volume because its cross-section is only nanometres wide. The cell must therefore solve a polymer-management problem: compacting and untangling the chromosome while keeping genes accessible for replication and transcription.4

4Where it is wrong

The 0.49% result is not the volume of the nucleoid. It assumes one genome equivalent of bare DNA packed without water or gaps. Real chromosomes are hydrated, supercoiled, bound by proteins and mixed with RNA and solvent; growing cells may also contain more than one genome equivalent.3,4 The estimate therefore answers only whether the DNA material fits, not how much cellular space the organized chromosome occupies.
