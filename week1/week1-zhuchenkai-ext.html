Week 1 — Extension: what the scaling step throws away is the shape parameter

Author: Zhu Chenkai · Week 1 (2026-09-07) · CCBS 2026 · on lecture 1, §6–§7

One sentence: Lecture 1's order-of-magnitude step silently sets the Fourier number κt/x² to 1; solving the same problem exactly gives 0.44 for a sphere and 1.59 for a slab, so the discarded factor is a shape parameter — the one thing §5 says a dimension count cannot supply — and the potato anchor's fifteen per cent is bought by the guess “call it 3 cm”, not by the formula.

The point I am adding

Lecture 1 §6 derives the heat equation honestly, then takes one more step:

Now take the order-of-magnitude step: replace each derivative by the variation over one characteristic scale, ∂T/∂t → T/t and ∂²T/∂x² → T/x². The temperature cancels and t ≈ x²/κ.

That is usually read as “drop the O(1) factors”. It is worth naming what it actually is. The heat equation has one dimensionless group, the Fourier number Fo = κt/a², and it is disposed as one.

However, it is a computable number that depends on the shape of the body. Which is what §5 warns about at the other end of the same lecture:

Quantities of the same dimension defeat it. … it is telling you that shape matters and units cannot decide it.

The prefactor the scaling step discards and the blind spot the Π theorem admits to are the same gap, reached from the two ends of lecture 1. The lecture keeps them in separate sections and never says so.

Work it

Take a body uniform at T₀ whose surface is stepped to Tₛ at t = 0, and follow the centre. With θ = (Tcentre−Tₛ)/(T₀−Tₛ) and Fo = κt/a², where a is the half-thickness of the slab or the radius of the cylinder or sphere, θ falls from 1 to 0 and separation of variables gives three standard series (Carslaw & Jaeger):

slab:   θ = (4/π) Σn≥0 (−1)n/(2n+1) · exp[−(2n+1)²π²Fo/4]
cyl:    θ = Σn≥1 2/(βnJ₁(βn)) · exp[−βn²Fo],   J₀(βn) = 0
sph:    θ = 2 Σn≥1 (−1)n+1 · exp[−n²π²Fo]

A threshold is needed, so take the one the potato implies: a wet skin holds the surface near 100 °C while the inside cooks, the potato starts at 20 °C, and done is a centre at 98 °C — that is θ = 0.025.

slab1.5931.261.6× too short
cylinder0.7190.851.4× too long
sphere0.4440.672.3× too long

A factor of 3.6 between the extremes, and the sign of the error flips between a slab and a sphere. The number the substitution guesses is not a universal constant a careful person could absorb once; it is a property of the shape, and no amount of counting units will produce it.

Why the potato model still worked

§7 bakes a potato: about 10 cm across, so a = 5 cm; “the heat has to reach the middle, so the distance is a few centimetres: call it 3 cm”; two hours; and κ = (0.03)²/7200 = 1.2×10⁻⁷ against water's 1.44×10⁻⁷. Three routes, one potato, the same two hours:

The third marker is the one that matters. Had the lecture read “the heat has to reach the middle” literally and used the radius, t ≈ x²/κ would have given 3.5×10⁻⁷ — 2.4× water. It gets away with it because “call it 3 cm” quietly supplies the missing prefactor: (3/5)² = 0.36, against the exact 0.444.

So “the distance the heat actually crosses” is not a geometric correction. It is the Fourier number in disguise: the effective distance is √Fo · a, which is 0.67a for a sphere, 0.85a for a cylinder, 1.26a for a slab.

The lecture informs that “what earns your trust is not the agreement but the fact that it does not depend on the guesses being good.” For this anchor it is the other way round: the guess is carrying the agreement. The robustness box still holds — 3–5 cm and 1–3 hours keeps water inside a factor-of-eight box — but because the box is wide, not because its centre is well placed.

What I would argue against

The threshold is mine. θ = 0.025 is my reading of a potato, and Fo depends on it — at θ = 0.1 the sphere gives 0.30 rather than 0.44. The conclusion does not move. Across thresholds from 0.5 to 0.01 the sphere runs 0.14 to 0.54 and never approaches 1, and the slab/sphere spread runs 2.7× to 3.7×. The spread is in the eigenvalues, not in my threshold.

My “exact” is exact for the wrong problem. A potato is not a sphere, and real baking has a moving evaporation front, latent heat and starch gelatinisation absorbing energy on the way. All lengthen the time. My 2.15 h is exact for the stated boundary-value problem and idealised for a potato, which is the same kind of claim I am criticising, one level further in.

And the generous reading, which is partly right. The lecture writes ≈, not =, and an argument landing within 2.4× has done what it promised. My complaint is narrower than “the line is wrong”: the substitution hides which quantity it is guessing, and that quantity has a name, a closed form, and a value units cannot see.

The code

Reproduces every number above and regenerates both figures. No numpy, no scipy, no matplotlib.

"""What the scaling substitution t ~ x^2/kappa throws away.

CCBS 2026, week 1 extension mini-essay.  Standard library only; no numpy,
no scipy, no matplotlib.  Run `python3 prefactor.py` to reproduce every
number quoted in the essay and to regenerate both figures.

Exact centre temperature after a step change of surface temperature, for the
three separable geometries (Carslaw & Jaeger, *Conduction of Heat in Solids*,
2nd ed. 1959: slab p.97 eq.(8), cylinder p.199 eq.(6), sphere p.233 eq.(4)).
With

    theta = (T_centre - T_surface) / (T_initial - T_surface)
    Fo    = kappa t / a^2        (a = half-thickness for the slab, radius otherwise)

theta = 1 at t = 0 and decays to 0.  The lecture's order-of-magnitude step,
d/dt -> 1/t and d2/dx2 -> 1/x2, is exactly the statement Fo := 1.
"""
import math

NT = 60  # series terms: plenty for Fo >= 0.05


# ---- Bessel J0, J1 from the integral representation (Watson, Sec. 2.2) ----
def bessel(n, x, m=2000):
    """J_n(x) = (1/pi) int_0^pi cos(n tau - x sin tau) dtau, by Simpson."""
    h = math.pi / m
    s = 0.0
    for i in range(m + 1):
        tau = i * h
        w = 1 if i in (0, m) else (4 if i % 2 else 2)
        s += w * math.cos(n * tau - x * math.sin(tau))
    return s * h / 3 / math.pi


def zeros_J0(k):
    """First k positive zeros of J0, bracketed by McMahon's asymptote."""
    out = []
    for i in range(1, k + 1):
        lo, hi = (i - 0.25) * math.pi - 0.35, (i - 0.25) * math.pi + 0.35
        flo = bessel(0, lo)
        while flo * bessel(0, hi) > 0:
            lo -= 0.1; hi += 0.1; flo = bessel(0, lo)
        for _ in range(80):
            mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
            if flo * bessel(0, mid) <= 0:
                hi = mid
            else:
                lo, flo = mid, bessel(0, mid)
        out.append(0.5 * (lo + hi))
    return out


_B = zeros_J0(NT)
_C = [2.0 / (b * bessel(1, b)) for b in _B]


def theta_slab(Fo):
    return 4 / math.pi * sum(
        (-1) ** n / (2 * n + 1) * math.exp(-((2 * n + 1) ** 2) * math.pi ** 2 * Fo / 4)
        for n in range(NT))


def theta_cyl(Fo):
    return sum(c * math.exp(-b * b * Fo) for b, c in zip(_B, _C))


def theta_sph(Fo):
    return 2 * sum((-1) ** (n + 1) * math.exp(-(n ** 2) * math.pi ** 2 * Fo)
                   for n in range(1, NT))


GEOM = {'slab': theta_slab, 'cylinder': theta_cyl, 'sphere': theta_sph}


def Fo_for(theta_target, geom):
    """Fourier number at which the centre first reaches theta_target."""
    f, lo, hi = GEOM[geom], 1e-4, 20.0
    for _ in range(200):
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        lo, hi = (mid, hi) if f(mid) > theta_target else (lo, mid)
    return 0.5 * (lo + hi)


# ------------------------------------------------------------- the potato --
# A whole potato in a hot oven.  Its skin is wet, so evaporation pins the
# surface near 100 C while the centre cooks; "done" is the centre reaching
# starch-gelatinisation temperature and then some -- call it 98 C.
T0, TS, TC = 20.0, 100.0, 98.0          # initial, surface, centre-when-done
THETA_DONE = (TC - TS) / (T0 - TS)      # = 0.025
A = 0.05                                # radius of a "10 cm across" potato, m
KW = 0.60 / (1000 * 4180)               # water diffusivity, lecture eq. (10)
T_OBS = 7200.0                          # "call it two hours"
X_LEC = 0.03                            # the lecture's "distance heat crosses"

FO = {g: Fo_for(THETA_DONE, g) for g in ('slab', 'cylinder', 'sphere')}


# ------------------------------------------------------------- figures -----
W1, H1 = 640, 300
L, R, TOP, BOT = 56, 16, 16, 44
PW, PH = W1 - L - R, H1 - TOP - BOT
FO_MAX, TH_LO = 2.5, 0.01

px = lambda fo: L + fo / FO_MAX * PW
py = lambda th: TOP + (-math.log10(th)) / (-math.log10(TH_LO)) * PH

STYLE = ('.ax{stroke:currentColor;stroke-width:1;opacity:.45;fill:none}'
         '.gr{stroke:currentColor;stroke-width:.5;opacity:.16;fill:none}'
         '.lb{font:11px system-ui,-apple-system,Segoe UI,Roboto,sans-serif;'
         'fill:currentColor;opacity:.75}'
         '.lbb{font:600 11.5px system-ui,-apple-system,Segoe UI,Roboto,sans-serif;'
         'fill:currentColor}'
         '.cv{fill:none;stroke-width:2.1}'
         '.naive{stroke:#c0392b;stroke-width:1.6;stroke-dasharray:5 3;fill:none}'
         '.done{stroke:currentColor;stroke-width:1;stroke-dasharray:2 3;'
         'opacity:.5;fill:none}')
COL = {'slab': '#1f4e79', 'cylinder': '#2e8b57', 'sphere': '#b8860b'}


def fig1(path):
    p = [f'<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 {W1} {H1}" '
         f'role="img" aria-label="Exact centre temperature versus Fourier '
         f'number for slab, cylinder and sphere"><style>{STYLE}</style>']
    for th in (1, .3, .1, .03, .01):
        y = py(th)
        p.append(f'<path class="gr" d="M{L} {y:.1f}H{L+PW}"/>')
        p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="{L-7}" y="{y+3.6:.1f}" '
                 f'text-anchor="end">{th:g}</text>')
    for fo in (0, .5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5):
        x = px(fo)
        p.append(f'<path class="gr" d="M{x:.1f} {TOP}V{TOP+PH}"/>')
        p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="{x:.1f}" y="{TOP+PH+16}" '
                 f'text-anchor="middle">{fo:g}</text>')
    p.append(f'<path class="ax" d="M{L} {TOP}V{TOP+PH}H{L+PW}"/>')
    p.append(f'<path class="done" d="M{L} {py(THETA_DONE):.1f}H{L+PW}"/>')
    p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="{L+PW-3}" y="{py(THETA_DONE)-5:.1f}" '
             f'text-anchor="end">centre &#8220;done&#8221;, &#952; = 0.025</text>')
    p.append(f'<path class="naive" d="M{px(1):.1f} {TOP}V{TOP+PH}"/>')
    p.append(f'<text class="lbb" x="{px(1)+6:.1f}" y="{TOP+13}" '
             f'fill="#c0392b">Fo := 1, what the substitution assumes</text>')
    for g in ('slab', 'cylinder', 'sphere'):
        f = GEOM[g]
        d, n = [], 260
        for i in range(n + 1):
            fo = 0.05 + (FO_MAX - 0.05) * i / n
            th = f(fo)
            if th < TH_LO * .9:
                break
            d.append(f'{"M" if not d else "L"}{px(fo):.2f} {py(max(th, TH_LO)):.2f}')
        p.append(f'<path class="cv" stroke="{COL[g]}" d="{"".join(d)}"/>')
        x, y = px(FO[g]), py(THETA_DONE)
        p.append(f'<circle cx="{x:.1f}" cy="{y:.1f}" r="3.6" fill="{COL[g]}"/>')
        p.append(f'<text class="lbb" x="{x:.1f}" y="{y+17:.1f}" '
                 f'text-anchor="middle" fill="{COL[g]}">{FO[g]:.2f}</text>')
    # legend: top right, the one corner every curve has left by Fo ~ 0.6
    lx = L + PW - 96
    for i, g in enumerate(('slab', 'cylinder', 'sphere')):
        yy = TOP + 46 + i * 17
        p.append(f'<path class="cv" stroke="{COL[g]}" d="M{lx} {yy}h20"/>')
        p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="{lx+26}" y="{yy+4}">{g}</text>')
    p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="{L+PW/2}" y="{H1-6}" text-anchor="middle">'
             f'Fourier number  Fo = &#954;t / a&#178;</text>')
    p.append(f'<text class="lb" x="-{TOP+PH/2}" y="13" text-anchor="middle" '
             f'transform="rotate(-90)">&#952;, centre</text>')
    p.append('</svg>')
    open(path, 'w').write('\n'.join(p))


W2, H2 = 640, 176
AX_Y, AL, AR = 96, 70, 600
RLO, RHI = 0.72, 3.0
rx = lambda r: AL + (math.log10(r) - math.log10(RLO)) / \
    (math.log10(RHI) - math.log10(RLO)) * (AR - AL)


def fig2(path):
    routes = [
        ('lecture: x = 3 cm, Fo := 1', X_LEC ** 2 / T_OBS, '#1f4e79', -1),
        ('exact sphere: a = 5 cm, Fo = 0.44', FO['sphere'] * A ** 2 / T_OBS, '#b8860b', 1),
        ('same formula, honest x = a = 5 cm', A ** 2 / T_OBS, '#c0392b', -1),
    ]
    p = [f'<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 {W2} {H2}" '

        p.append(f'<path class="ax" d="M{x:.1f} {AX_Y}v5"/>')

    # How much does the answer depend on MY choice of "done"?  At large Fo each
