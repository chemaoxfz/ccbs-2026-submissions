When 1 nM often means zero

Zixuan Wang · Week 1 (2026-09-07) · CCBS 2026

One sentence: Concentration alone does not determine signal reliability: under an independent Poisson null model, the product cV sets both the chance that a compartment contains no molecule and its relative copy-number fluctuation, so the same 1 nM signal can be mostly absent at 1 fL but smooth at 1000 fL.

The point I am adding

Lecture 2 gives a conversion worth remembering: one molecule in a 1-fL compartment is approximately 1 nM [1]. I want to run that statement in the opposite direction. If a nominal concentration is 1 nM, how many whole molecules represent it, and when is “concentration” a useful continuous variable rather than an average over integer counts?

The conversion itself is exact bookkeeping. If a compartment of volume V has concentration c, its expected molecule count is

λ = cVNA,   or, in convenient units,   λ = 0.602 cnMVfL.

Here NA is Avogadro's constant and λ is an ensemble mean, not a fractional molecule inside one cell. At 1 nM and 1 fL, λ = 0.602; at the same concentration and 1000 fL, λ = 602. The concentration step created by one additional molecule is Δc = 1/(NAV): 1.66 nM at 1 fL but only 0.00166 nM at 1000 fL. This discreteness follows from counting alone and requires no noise model [1,2].

Work it: present is not the same as precise

Now add a deliberately simple model. Suppose molecules are independent and well mixed, as in an ideal open compartment or a constant-birth, first-order-death process at steady state. The copy number is then Poisson distributed. Its two useful statistics are

P0 = exp(−λ),     CV = σ/λ = 1/√λ.

P0 is the probability that an independent compartment or snapshot contains zero molecules. CV is the standard deviation divided by the mean. For 1 nM in 1 fL, the model predicts P0 = 54.8% and CV = 129%. For 1 nM in 1000 fL, the zero-count probability is negligible and CV = 4.1%.

54.8%zero molecules at 1 nM and 1 fL
4.1%Poisson CV at 1 nM and 1000 fL
21.7×more copies needed for precision than mere presence

I defined two operational standards. “Present” means at least a 99% probability of one or more molecules, so exp(−λ) ≤ 0.01 and λ ≥ 4.605. “Precise” means Poisson CV ≤ 10%, so λ ≥ 100. Using the unit conversion above gives

99% non-empty:  cnMVfL ≥ 7.65;     CV ≤ 10%:  cnMVfL ≥ 166.

The thresholds differ by a factor of 100/4.605 = 21.7. A molecular species can therefore be almost certainly present while still being a poor continuous signal.

Python code that reproduces the numbers and boundary curves 
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Because c is in nM and V is in fL:
# lambda = (c * 1e-9 mol/L)(V * 1e-15 L)(N_A molecules/mol)
NA = 6.02214076e23
factor = NA * 1e-24                 # 0.602214076

def statistics(c_nM, V_fL):
    lam = factor * c_nM * V_fL
    p_zero = np.exp(-lam)
    cv = 1 / np.sqrt(lam)
    return lam, p_zero, cv

for V in [1, 10, 100, 1000]:
    lam, p0, cv = statistics(1, V)
    print(V, lam, p0, cv)

# Author-chosen operational criteria
lambda_present = -np.log(0.01)      # P0 = 1%
lambda_precise = 1 / 0.10**2        # CV = 10%

V = np.logspace(-1, 4, 400)
c_present = lambda_present / (factor * V)
c_precise = lambda_precise / (factor * V)

plt.loglog(V, c_present, label="P0 = 1%")
plt.loglog(V, c_precise, label="CV = 10%")
plt.scatter([1, 1000], [1, 1])
plt.xlabel("Compartment volume V (fL)")
plt.ylabel("Concentration c (nM)")
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Lecture 1 adds one more view. For geometrically similar compartments, V ∝ L3. At fixed concentration, λ ∝ L3 and Poisson CV ∝ L−3/2. Shrinking linear size tenfold therefore reduces the expected count 1000-fold and raises relative counting noise by √1000 ≈ 32. This is the same concentration, but not the same physical representation.

What I would argue against / where this breaks

The conversion λ = cVNA is bookkeeping; the Poisson prediction is conditional. Fixed concentration alone does not imply Poisson counts. Transcriptional bursting, shared upstream fluctuations, cell-cycle variation and changing cell volume can produce fluctuations larger than this baseline. Single-cell experiments have separated intrinsic from extrinsic expression noise [4], and simultaneous measurements of transcript number and cell volume show both approximate concentration homeostasis and additional biological variability [3]. Conversely, feedback or tighter copy-number control can reduce noise below the simple model [5].

The relevant V may also be a local compartment rather than the whole cell, and a downstream system can average repeated observations over time. Finally, my 99% presence and 10% CV lines are definitions chosen to make two questions comparable, not fitness thresholds measured in an organism. The robust conclusion is narrower: in the Poisson baseline, cV selects the discreteness regime; departures from that baseline are where biological regulation and spatial organization become interesting.
