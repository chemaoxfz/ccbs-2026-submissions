Week 2 — Extension: When the cell is crowded

Author: 20261203497-fanlipei · Week 2 (2026-09-07) · CCBS 2026

One sentence: The diffusion-limited rate from Lecture 2 is a dilute-solution ceiling; macromolecular crowding changes both sides of the encounter: it slows motion, but it also leaves less free volume.

The point I am adding

Lecture 2 estimates an encounter rate with the Smoluchowski form k ≈ 4π(DA + DB)R. That derivation treats the solvent as a uniform liquid. Cytoplasm is not: proteins, nucleic acids and ribosomes occupy roughly 20–40% of its volume. A molecule therefore moves through a fluid that is both more viscous and geometrically obstructed.

A useful first model is to separate these effects. Let λ be the viscosity slowdown, so Dcrowded ≈ Ddilute/λ. Let p be the fraction of attempted encounters that are geometrically accessible. Then

kcrowded ≈ kdilute × p / λ

This does not replace a measurement: it tells us what to measure. Crowding can increase local concentration and sometimes speed a reaction through excluded volume, while the same crowding can reduce translational diffusion. The net effect is therefore not determined by volume fraction alone.

Work it

Use deliberately coarse numbers. Suppose crowding makes a small protein diffuse three times more slowly (λ = 3), and steric exclusion leaves 70% of nominal encounters usable (p = 0.7). The encounter ceiling becomes 0.7/3 ≈ 0.23 of the dilute value. If the dilute estimate was 108 M−1 s−1, the crowded estimate is about 2 × 107 M−1 s−1.

What I would argue against / where this breaks

It would be wrong to conclude that crowding always slows biology by fourfold. The equation assumes independent diffusion, a single effective viscosity, and a reaction radius that does not change. Real cytoplasm is heterogeneous and transient: binding to a scaffold can reduce a molecule's search volume but raise its local concentration, and weak attractions can make an encounter last longer. A better experiment therefore compares the same reaction in a reconstituted crowded mixture and in dilute buffer, measuring both D and k rather than inferring one from the other.
