Where an RDC spends its radioactive lifetime

Xinyu Wu (吴新宇) · Week 4 · CCBS 2026 · 26 September 2026

A candidate with lower peak tumor activity can produce more nuclear decays in the tumor if it remains there longer.

The question behind an uptake measurement

When comparing radionuclide–drug conjugates (RDCs), I would naturally favor the candidate that reaches the tumor sooner and achieves higher uptake. Yet an RDC keeps decaying throughout delivery and residence. Lecture 8 connects state populations to output fluxes and closes their dynamics with input and loss [1]. Here I apply that structure to surviving radioactive nuclei in the tumor, then integrate their local decay flux to compare candidates.

Arrival, decay and escape

Take two hypothetical 177Lu-labeled candidates with the same injected activity and one nucleus per labeled molecule. The physical half-life is 6.647 days [2], so λ=ln(2)/6.647 d−1. Approximate delivery as a single arrival at time td. A fraction f of injected molecules would arrive if physical decay were switched off; therefore the surviving fraction arriving is f e−λtd.

Biological loss has rate k=ln(2)/Tbio. Assume first-order loss, stable labeling before decay, and no re-entry. Tbio describes loss from the tumor compartment and can include several biological processes. With NT surviving nuclei in the tumor and JT(t) their arrival flux, the state balance and local output are

dNT/dt = JT(t) − (λ+k)NT,
vlocal(t) = λNT(t).

JT counts nuclei arriving per day; λNT and kNT count losses through decay and departure. The single arrival adds N0f e−λtd nuclei at td, with JT=0 afterward. Solving the resulting loss equation gives, for t≥td,

NT(t)/N0 = f e−λtd e−(λ+k)(t−td).

N0 is the initially injected radioactive-nucleus count. The time integral of vlocal, divided by N0, is our comparison metric: the fraction of injected nuclei that decay inside the tumor.

PT = ∫td∞ λNT(t)/N0 dt
= f e−λtd × λ/(λ+k).

The first factor counts surviving arrivals. The second is the probability that a nucleus decays before it leaves the tumor. My null ranking uses peak tumor activity alone; the calculation tests whether that ranking survives integration over time.

Among surviving arrivals, the probability of decaying before biological loss is 13.1% for A and 37.6% for B. Longer retention more than compensates for B's lower delivery fraction and greater decay loss during transit.

What I would measure

Real uptake is spread over time, and washout may have several phases. Replacing the arrival pulse with an influx curve would extend this calculation. Normal-organ activity, tumor mass and radiation transport are also needed to estimate absorbed dose and therapeutic benefit. With the same isotope, tumor geometry and absorbed-energy fraction, tumor self-dose scales with the integrated local decay count. Clinical dosimetry methods likewise integrate time–activity curves before applying dose-conversion factors [3].

For candidate selection, I would measure tumor activity during uptake and at later times that resolve washout, then compare the curve areas at matched injected activity. A candidate with a modest peak and sustained retention deserves to remain in that comparison.

Calculation and reproducibility

The upper panel shows actual activity relative to activity at injection, without decay correction. Its vertical jumps reflect the assumed single arrival. The lower panel shows ∫λNT/N0 dt, multiplied by 1,000; labels give the analytic infinite-time limits. Time and rate units are days and day−1.

The two closed-form integrals were checked by independent midpoint quadrature at two step sizes, with a far-tail correction. A post-arrival balance checks remaining nuclei plus local decays plus biological departures against the initial tumor pool. The model does not specify the fate of nuclei outside this compartment; they may later decay elsewhere or be excreted.

Python 3 standard library only. Save and run the following as rdc_calculations.py to reproduce the SVG and CSV.

"""Illustrative RDC delivery/retention model, standard-library Python 3.
Run: python rdc_calculations.py
Only the Lu-177 physical half-life is sourced; all delivery parameters are hypothetical.
Time unit: day. Activity curves are actual, NOT decay corrected, relative to injected activity.
"""
import csv
import math
from pathlib import Path

HALF_LIFE_DAYS = 6.647
LAM = math.log(2)/HALF_LIFE_DAYS
CASES = [('A', .04, .25, 1., '#c65c2e'), ('B', .03, 1., 4., '#087b83')]

def peak(f, arrival):
    return f*math.exp(-LAM*arrival)

def activity(t, f, arrival, bio_half):
    if t < arrival:
        return 0.
    return peak(f,arrival)*math.exp(-(LAM+math.log(2)/bio_half)*(t-arrival))

def local_decay_fraction(f, arrival, bio_half):
    return peak(f,arrival)*LAM/(LAM+math.log(2)/bio_half)

def accumulated(t, f, arrival, bio_half):
    if t < arrival:
        return 0.
    k = LAM+math.log(2)/bio_half
    return local_decay_fraction(f,arrival,bio_half)*(-math.expm1(-k*(t-arrival)))

def verify():
    # Independent midpoint integration of lambda*N_T/N0, including analytic far tail.
    for name,f,td,h,_ in CASES:
        exact=local_decay_fraction(f,td,h)
        errs=[]
        for step in [.002,.001]:
            steps=round(80/step)
            numerical=LAM*step*sum(activity(td+(i+.5)*step,f,td,h) for i in range(steps))
            tail=exact*math.exp(-(LAM+math.log(2)/h)*80)
            errs.append(abs(numerical+tail-exact))
        assert errs[1]<1e-9 and errs[1]<errs[0]
        # At a chosen time, remaining + locally decayed + biologically departed = arrivals.
        t=td+3
        k=math.log(2)/h
        remaining=activity(t,f,td,h)
        decayed=accumulated(t,f,td,h)
        departed=decayed*k/LAM
        assert abs(remaining+decayed+departed-peak(f,td))<1e-12
        assert 0 < exact < peak(f,td) < f
        print(f'{name}: peak activity / injected activity={peak(f,td):.9f}; local decays / injected nuclei={exact:.9f}; decays per 1000={1000*exact:.4f}')
    ratio=local_decay_fraction(*CASES[1][1:4])/local_decay_fraction(*CASES[0][1:4])
    print(f'B/A local decay count={ratio:.6f}')
    print('PASS: independent integration, refinement and post-arrival mass balance.')

        d=f'M{x(0):.2f},{ya(0):.2f} L{x(td):.2f},{ya(0):.2f} '
        d+=' '.join(f'L{x(t):.2f},{ya(activity(t,f,td,h)):.2f}' for t in ts)
        parts.append(f'<path d="{d}" fill="none" stroke="{color}" stroke-width="2.7"/>')
        d=f'M{x(0):.2f},{yc(0):.2f} '+' '.join(f'L{x(t):.2f},{yc(accumulated(t,f,td,h)):.2f}' for t in ts)

        parts.append(f'<text x="450" y="{yc(accumulated(14,f,td,h))-12:.1f}" fill="{color}" font-weight="bold">{name} → {1000*local_decay_fraction(f,td,h):.2f} at long times</text>')
    return '\n'.join(parts+['</g></svg>'])

if __name__ == '__main__':
    verify()
    out=Path(__file__).resolve().parent
    (out/'rdc-decay-retention.svg').write_text(make_svg(),encoding='utf-8')
    with (out/'rdc-model.csv').open('w',encoding='utf-8',newline='') as f:
        writer=csv.writer(f)
        writer.writerow(['time_days','A_activity_fraction','B_activity_fraction','A_cumulative_decay_fraction','B_cumulative_decay_fraction'])
        for i in range(1401):
            t=i/100
            writer.writerow([t,*[activity(t,*case[1:4]) for case in CASES],*[accumulated(t,*case[1:4]) for case in CASES]])
